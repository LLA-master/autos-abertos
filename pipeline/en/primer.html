Brazil primer: the court, the cops, the regulators and the jargon

What a reader outside Brazil needs in order to follow this record. General background on how Brazilian institutions work; where the text refers to this case, it says only what the case file itself says. Portuguese terms are kept in italics because they recur on every page of the record.

1. Why a bank case sits at the Supreme Court

Brazil's Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF, Supreme Federal Court) is both the constitutional court and the trial court for a short list of officials who hold what Brazilians call foro por prerrogativa de função, a privileged venue: sitting members of Congress, cabinet ministers and a few others can only be investigated and tried by the STF. When such a person surfaces in an investigation, the whole case, or the part touching that person, moves up to the Court. The Court can also pull a case up through a reclamação (RCL), a constitutional complaint asserting that a lower court is trespassing on the STF's jurisdiction. Both mechanisms appear in this record.

The Court has eleven justices, called ministros in Portuguese, appointed by the President with Senate approval and serving until the age of 75. It sits as a full bench (Plenário) or as two panels of five, the Primeira and Segunda Turma. Every case is assigned by lot to one justice, the relator, translated here as reporting justice. The relator runs the case day to day and can decide urgent measures alone, in a single-justice ruling (decisão monocrática). Such rulings are then put to the panel, which either confirms or overturns them; the record calls that confirmation a referendo. A change of relator by redistribution happened in this case, and both panels' names appear in the filings.

2. The three kinds of proceeding in this record

INQ, inquérito
A criminal investigation supervised by the Court. The Federal Police investigates; the reporting justice authorises intrusive measures; the Prosecutor General decides whether to indict. An inquiry is not a charge: nobody in an INQ has been accused of anything by a prosecutor yet.
PET, petição
An ancillary or precautionary proceeding. In the STF's practice, requests for searches, detentions, asset freezes and similar measures are docketed as separate petitions attached to an inquiry. That is why the "main" proceeding here, Pet 15.556, is technically a petition and not the inquiry itself. Pet 15.198 is the docket where the second phase of the operation was requested and decided.
RCL, reclamação
A constitutional complaint. Used here to bring a São Paulo investigation up to the STF on the argument that it touched the Court's exclusive jurisdiction.

Brazilian docket numbers use a dot as a thousands separator: "Pet 15.556" is petition number fifteen thousand five hundred and fifty-six. This site writes the same number as "PET 15556". Inside each proceeding, every docket entry carries a sequence number, seq, which is also the beginning of the file name in the STF's public package; the "Where to check" tables on this site cite proceeding, seq and page.

3. Who does what

Polícia Federal (PF), the Federal Police
Investigates federal crimes, including crimes against the financial system and money laundering. Under Brazilian procedure the police cannot search, detain or freeze assets on their own authority outside a crime in progress: they file a representação, an application, and a judge decides. A delegado is a police chief with a law degree who signs those applications. "Operação Compliance Zero" is the PF's name for this investigation.
Procuradoria-Geral da República (PGR), the Prosecutor General's Office
The head of the federal prosecution service, the Ministério Público Federal. Before the STF, only the PGR can bring charges. Before deciding a police request, the reporting justice normally gives the PGR vista, a referral for an opinion within a deadline. A PGR manifestação is that opinion; a denúncia would be an indictment, which is a later stage than anything summarised on this site.
The defence
Lawyers are identified in the record by their bar registration (OAB). They file petições asking, for instance, for the return of seized property or the revocation of detention. Their filings are part of the archive, but the precautionary rulings summarised here were, as a rule, issued before the defence was heard.
Banco Central do Brasil (BCB), the Central Bank
Licenses and supervises banks. Its bank-supervision department appears in the record under the acronym Desup. When a bank is judged unrecoverable, the Central Bank can order its liquidação extrajudicial, an extrajudicial liquidation: the bank is closed and wound up by a liquidator appointed by the Central Bank, outside the courts.
Fundo Garantidor de Créditos (FGC)
The private deposit-guarantee fund financed by the banks themselves. It repays depositors and holders of bank deposit certificates up to a per-person, per-institution ceiling when a bank is liquidated. Because of it, high-yield bank paper sold to retail investors through brokerage platforms carries an implicit safety net, which is part of the background the record describes.
Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM)
The securities regulator, Brazil's counterpart to the SEC. Supervises investment funds, asset managers and public companies.
Controladoria-Geral da União (CGU)
The federal government's internal-control and anti-corruption agency. Among other things it runs disciplinary proceedings against federal civil servants, which is the capacity in which it appears here.
Tribunais Regionais Federais (TRF)
The federal appellate courts. TRF1 sits in Brasília and covers the Federal District and much of the centre and north; TRF3 sits in São Paulo. A Vara Criminal Federal is a first-instance federal criminal court; the record includes a copy of a criminal action from the 6th Federal Criminal Court of São Paulo.
CPMI, Comissão Parlamentar Mista de Inquérito
A joint congressional inquiry committee, with power to summon witnesses. When a person under investigation is summoned, the Court may issue a salvo-conduto, a safe-conduct order guaranteeing that the person may remain silent and will not be arrested for doing so.

4. The measures a judge can order before any trial

Busca e apreensão, search and seizure
A warrant to search premises and seize documents, devices, cash and vehicles. Seized property is inventoried and can be released back on request (restituição de coisas apreendidas).
Prisão temporária, temporary detention
A fixed-term detention, requested by the police during an investigation, to secure evidence or prevent flight. It expires on its own unless extended.
Prisão preventiva, pre-trial detention
Open-ended detention that the judge must justify under article 312 of the Code of Criminal Procedure: evidence that a crime occurred and indications of authorship (fumus commissi delicti), plus a concrete danger created by the person's liberty (periculum libertatis), for one of three stated purposes: protecting public or economic order, protecting the investigation, or ensuring that the law can be enforced. The judge must also explain why less severe alternatives would not do. The ruling summarised in the Pet 15.556 chronicles works through exactly those steps.
Medidas cautelares diversas, alternatives to detention
Electronic ankle monitor (tornozeleira eletrônica), surrender of passport, ban on contact with other suspects, suspension from a job or from company management.
Sequestro, bloqueio, asset freeze
Freezing of bank accounts, funds or property up to an amount the judge sets, so that they are available for eventual forfeiture or compensation.
Cognição sumária, summary assessment
The standard for all of the above: the judge decides on the probability of the allegations, on the file as it stands and usually without hearing the defence. It is not a finding of guilt, and Brazil's Constitution (article 5, LVII) presumes innocence until a conviction becomes final.
Sigilo, sealing
Investigations run under seal. The seal can be lifted in whole or in part by the reporting justice; that is what happened on 10 September 2026, and the resulting public package is the sole source of this site. Even after unsealing, the STF's case headers still read "under seal", so this site rebuilt the list of parties from the body of the filings.

5. The financial instruments that keep coming up

CDB, Certificado de Depósito Bancário
A bank-issued time deposit, the basic retail funding instrument of a Brazilian bank, covered by the FGC guarantee and widely sold through investment platforms.
FIDC, Fundo de Investimento em Direitos Creditórios
A receivables fund: a vehicle that buys loans, invoices or other credit rights. Its shares are called quotas; a fund with a cotista único has a single quotaholder, which is the kind of structure the police filings describe at length.
FIP, Fundo de Investimento em Participações
A private-equity fund that holds stakes in companies.
DTVM, Distribuidora de Títulos e Valores Mobiliários
A securities dealer licensed by the Central Bank and the CVM. A DTVM often acts as the administrator of investment funds, the entity legally responsible for the fund, while a separate gestora (asset manager) makes the investment decisions.
CNPJ and CPF
The company registry number and the individual taxpayer number. Every Brazilian company and every person has one; the record is full of them. This site publishes company numbers and never publishes individual ones.
Money
Amounts are in Brazilian reais (R$). Over 2025 and 2026 one US dollar bought roughly five to six reais; check the rate of the day before converting a figure. Brazilian usage writes R$ 1.000.000,00 for one million.

6. How to read a Brazilian court filing

  • Parties and companies are written in CAPITAL LETTERS; that convention is what this site's name extraction relies on.
  • A single-justice ruling opens with a summary of the request (relatório), then the reasoning (fundamentação), then the operative part (dispositivo). The chronicles on Pet 15.556 follow that anatomy.
  • Dates are day/month/year. "3.3.2026" is 3 March 2026.
  • "Ministro" before a name means a Supreme Court justice, not a member of the cabinet. "Desembargador" is an appellate judge; "juiz" a first-instance judge; "delegado" a police chief; "procurador" a federal prosecutor.
  • Nothing in the record summarised here is a conviction. Every allegation belongs to whoever made it, and this site keeps that attribution: "the PF states", "the PGR argues", "the ruling says".
