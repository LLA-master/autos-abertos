A disclosure order is a request with a list. This chapter is about the list in Petition 15.478, about what the police say each name did, and about the path the request travelled between February and June 2026. It is the most legal chapter of the series and the site has tried to keep it readable for those outside the trade; where it failed, it says so.

## The list

The police asked for the lifting of bank secrecy, from 1 January 2024 to 13 December 2025, and of tax secrecy, up to 31 December 2025, for thirteen names. Nine people: Daniel Bueno Vorcaro, Augusto Ferreira Lima, Luiz Antonio Bull, Alberto Felix de Oliveira Neto, Ângelo Antônio Ribeiro da Silva, André Felipe de Oliveira Seixas Maia, Henrique Souza e Silva Peretto, Paulo Henrique Bezerra Rodrigues Costa and Dario Oswaldo Garcia Junior. Four companies: Tirreno, Viking Participações and two companies called Terra Firme, one of construction and property development, the other "of Bahia". A tenth name appears in the text as a signatory of contracts, "Allan da Silva Machado", but is not on the list; the site mentions him because the request mentions him, and nothing more.

The request organises the thirteen into three "cells" (*núcleos*), and to each person attributes articles of the law on crimes against the financial system: fraudulent and reckless management, inducing a public body into error, issuing a false instrument, fraud against supervision, omission in accounting statements, plus criminal organisation. The reader should note that "attributing articles" in a request is not accusing; it is telling the judge which crime the police are investigating in relation to each person, so that he can assess whether the disclosure is pertinent. The document explains, with the candour this kind of filing allows, why the law reaches the controller: "under Law 7.492/86, article 25, the controller and the managers of a financial institution, the directors being so considered, are criminally liable". "That being so, there is no way to detach Daniel Vorcaro from the criminal practices observed on the part of the group's Directors, since he is the controller of Banco Master." It is liability by position, added, in his case, to what the phone showed.

On Cartos, the police went beyond the two partners: "there is ample evidence that Tirreno and Cartos are one and the same, Tirreno being a mere intermediary (*pessoa interposta*) of that financial institution": the partners are the same, the contract with Master says Cartos controls Tirreno, there is an operating agreement between the two, and "BRB states that the credits were created by 20 correspondents of the company Cartos". On BRB, the argument is the date: on 10 February 2025, the public bank had signed with the Central Bank a commitment agreement acknowledging "failures in the assessment of the credits"; "even though already aware of the failures in its capacity to assess credits, BRB chooses to acquire 12.2 billion in CCBs from Banco Master, 4.05 billion of them after the BCB's questioning". And the signature: the contract to repurchase the portfolios, with Tirreno's promise to return R$ 7 billion, "was physically signed by BRB's President and by its Executive Finance Director".

## Why lift secrecy

The general justification fits in one sentence: "to identify all the persons, natural and legal, who benefited from the improper payments". The request says the disclosures "reinforce the implementation and success of the freezing and seizure measures already granted", that is, they serve to find assets; that they make it possible "to demonstrate the growth in the suspects' wealth during the period in which Banco Master was reselling rotten portfolios to Tirreno"; and that Tirreno is "a phantom company, created out of backdated contracts, making it indispensable to consolidate its tax and banking data". There are twenty pages of case law on secrecy, which the site sums up in one line: no right is absolute, and disclosure by court order, limited in time and in persons, is admitted "at any stage of the inquiry".

The operational part is revealing of what a disclosure order is today. The police ask that the justice's Registry consult the Financial System Customer Register to find out at which banks each name holds an account; that the banks send the data "in the layout defined in Circular Letter 3454/2010" of the Central Bank, through a program called Simba, the Bank Transactions Investigation System, under a "case identifier code"; that the Central Bank send "all the records in the Foreign Exchange System" for the suspects; and that the tax authority deliver income-tax returns, the "integrated dossier", the "e-Financeira", property transactions "since 1980", cash transactions, crypto-assets, invoices, infraction notices, accounting books, all "in selectable PDF format, without watermark", "organised by CPF/CNPJ". It also asks the São Paulo state tax authority for donations, inheritances and invoices. A reader from the last century would picture a disclosure order as a court officer opening a safe. It is a transfer of files with a hash.

## The opinion

Justice André Mendonça sent the file to the PGR for comment "urgently" on 24 February 2026, with a five-day deadline. The Prosecutor General, Paulo Gonet, replied on 14 March, in eleven pages, nine of which summarise the request with the messages transcribed. The opinion's own part is short. The lifting "rests on the need to identify the origin and destination of the funds moved by the criminal organisation, as well as to ascertain its actual mode of operation". "The progress of the investigation depends on access to the data sought." The police "indicated the connection of each of the entities to the investigation and delimited their probable participation in the offence". And, on hearing those affected first, which the Code of Criminal Procedure as a rule requires: "given the nature and urgency of the measures requested, the application of deferred adversarial proceedings proves indispensable, since notifying the subjects of the request to present a prior response would prejudice the line of investigation". Translation: to warn is to warn them to hide. The prosecution service "agrees, on the terms specified in this note, with the request".

## The ruling

The ruling is dated 22 June 2026. Between the favourable opinion and the ruling a hundred days went by, and the docket records nothing in that interval. The site does not know the reason; it knows that a measure requested "urgently" in February was granted at the end of June, and that the police, in the meantime, had already requested and obtained other disclosure orders in other petitions, as the earlier series show. It may be that this one fell behind precisely because it was broad; it may be that it waited for the forensic reports. The record does not say.

The ruling runs to 21 pages and adopts the police thesis in full, with the verbal caution the stage demands. "There are present, on a preliminary assessment (*juízo de delibação*), sufficient indications of the crime and its authorship." The scheme, "according to the police request and the MPF's opinion", would consist of "creating a documentary appearance of legitimacy for problematic portfolios, pressing for their settlement by BRB, answering regulatory demands with forged or incomplete documentation and keeping the flow of acquisitions going even after warnings from the Central Bank". The justice then does what the police had done: a summary per name, thirteen numbered paragraphs from 13.1 to 13.13, each ending with "in theory" (*em tese*). Daniel Vorcaro, "controller/owner of Banco Master and central beneficiary of the scheme". Augusto Lima, "central agent of interface with BRB". Bull, whose "role does not appear as merely formal". Alberto Felix, "robust indications of tampering with records". Ângelo Ribeiro, "co-author of the fabrication of an artificial statement". Seixas Maia, "is not that of a mere formal signatory". Peretto, "unrestricted knowledge". Paulo Henrique Costa, "a picture of grave connivance". Dario Garcia Junior, "conscious participation in keeping up the acquisition of assets known to be problematic". Tirreno, "operational vehicle of the fraud". Viking, included "for being a legal entity linked to the members", "the disclosure being recommended for the purposes of tracing and of confirming or refuting that link". The Terra Firme companies, "possible recipients of diverted funds".

The summary on Viking deserves attention, because it is the only point at which the ruling acknowledges that the disclosure serves for "confirming or refuting": the company joins the list without anyone saying what it did, only that it is "linked". It is the logic of an asset investigation, and the ruling accepts it.

The operative part grants everything, extends the bank disclosure to 31 December 2025, a little beyond what was asked, and adds a clause that turns this petition into a conduit for the others: "the sharing of the confidential data supplied by virtue of this ruling with other Federal Police operations related to 'Operation Compliance Zero' is hereby authorised". The data the banks send here can be used in any sister petition. On 2 July, the PGR acknowledged receipt. The letters went out on 23 June.

## What the ruling does not discuss

Two things. The first is the defence: there is none, and there could not be, because the procedure is that of deferred adversarial proceedings; but that means the messages transcribed in the previous chapter reached the Supreme Court and were adopted by it without anyone, on the other side, saying what they mean in context. The second is proportion: nine people and four companies, two years, every account, every tax return, foreign exchange, crypto, property, and the possibility of passing all of it on to other investigations. The ruling says the measure is "proportionate in the strict sense, because limited to specified persons, to a set period and to an investigated fact of great gravity". It is true, and it is also the description of a complete sweep. The court held that the gravity justifies it. The site notes that the same sentence would fit any disclosure order, and that what makes it acceptable, or not, is what comes afterwards: whether the data show what the police said they would show. That is the subject of the last chapter.

> Reading rule number twenty-six: in a disclosure order, "in theory" (*em tese*) is the most frequent phrase and the most important one. It appears thirteen times in the ruling, once per name, and it is the difference between investigating and accusing.
