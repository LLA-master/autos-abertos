The ruling that put Banco Master's directors in custody runs to 26 pages, was signed at 3:29 pm on 17 November 2025 by substitute federal judge Ricardo Augusto Soares Leite, of the 10th Federal Criminal Court of Brasília, and was executed the following morning. It is, alongside the Supreme Court's ruling of March 2026, the other great detention order of the case, and it is quite different from it: shorter, more bank-minded, more open at the edges. This chapter is about it and about the ten days that followed.

## Who and why

The judge ordered the pre-trial detention of Daniel Bueno Vorcaro, Augusto Ferreira Lima, Luiz Antonio Bull, Alberto Felix de Oliveira Neto and Ângelo Antônio Ribeiro da Silva; the temporary detention, for three days, of André Felipe de Oliveira Seixas Maia and Henrique Souza e Silva Peretto; and the removal for 60 days of BRB's president and finance director. To each he assigned a role, always drawn from the police request, and always with the formula "the police authority correctly imputes".

Vorcaro, "president and director", allegedly had "the lead in the acts of recalcitrance in placing on the market insubstantial or 'rotten' credit instruments, securities and loan portfolios". Augusto Lima was said to be "a partner and Director of Banco Master" who "indirectly controls two associations of civil servants of the State of Bahia", holding a power of attorney "to deal with financial institutions", and whose participation was said to be "indispensable and relevant" in raising the money from BRB. Bull and Ângelo Ribeiro, directors, were said to be signatories of the contracts with Tirreno and of the letter to the Central Bank "in which it indicates the originator of the loans, pointing to the two associations". Alberto Felix "appeared as Director" and signed "the partnership contract between Master and Tirreno". Seixas Maia, a former Master employee and Tirreno's director, was said to be "the operational core responsible for carrying out the serial production of false documents". Peretto, a partner in Cartos, allegedly paid in Tirreno's capital.

The reasoning on the need for detention is the passage where the ruling exposes itself most. The judge writes that "only an abrupt curtailment of liberty can produce any effectiveness", that "it would be naive to believe that at liberty they would not act in the same way to conceal or disguise earlier crimes", that "it would be frivolous naivety to believe that they could only commit unlawful acts from within a financial institution", and that there is a "need for an effective response by the State". He cites the "magnitude of the harm", which "may reach the figure of 18 billion reais and compromise the liquidity of a state-owned bank", and the "various press reports attached by the police authority". One sentence, probably by a slip, speaks of restraining "the disposition of the directors of the Central Bank", where it meant Banco Master. This text records the slip because the defence would later record that the ruling had been written "docilely" (*docilmente*) over the police request.

## The order to the Central Bank

The most unusual item in the operative part is not a detention. It is item 4.1: "given the detention of the Directors of Banco Master and the precautionary removal of the executives of Banco Regional de Brasília, the Central Bank of Brazil shall appoint an intervenor to manage the said financial institutions for a period of 60 days". A first-instance judge directing the Central Bank to intervene in a state-owned bank and a private one. The judge also ordered that the detainees be brought before him the next day, at 2 pm, for a custody hearing, and authorised the sharing of evidence with the oversight bodies and, "by serendipity", with other inquiries.

The Central Bank answered the same day, in its own way. On 18 November 2025, while the warrants were being executed, it ordered "the liquidation of Banco Master S/A, Banco Master de Investimento S/A, Banco Letsbank S/A and Master S/A Corretora", and a special regime at Banco Master Múltiplo. The sentence is in the TRF1 (the federal appeals court for the 1st region) ruling of two days later, which cites it as a new fact: "the Central Bank of Brazil decreed yesterday". As for BRB, the prosecution service went back to the judge on 24 November, "after a meeting held today with representatives of BACEN", to say that the state-owned bank "does not present a liquidity crisis, particularly given the possibility of a capital increase by the majority shareholder, the Federal District", and that intervention "does not appear necessary". It asked instead for an audit. The judge "granted it in full": an audit of the 2025 operations within 20 days, of the 2024 ones within 60, and the sharing of evidence with the MPF's civil offices and with the Federal District prosecution service, "for the recovery of public assets and possible liability for administrative improbity". The intervention lasted six days on paper and none in practice.

## The defence's arguments

The record of the detention request, copied to the STF, preserves the requests for revocation the defences made that week, and the site reports them because they are the only contrary voice that exists at this stage. Vorcaro, through his lawyers, argued that the group's liquidation had stripped him of "any position within the National Financial System", that the asset freeze removed the risk of concealment, and that the trip he was about to make when arrested "arose from negotiations to be held in the United Arab Emirates for the sale of Banco Master to the consortium formed by Fictor Holding Financeira", "widely reported". The accommodation, he said, was booked only until 22 November; the flight plan showed Malta as a stopover "owing to the need to refuel in order to fly from Guarulhos to Dubai". On 25 November he filed a Central Bank document confirming a video call on the morning of 17 November, from 1:30 pm to 2:10 pm, with the director of supervision, the head of Desup and the deputy head, in which Vorcaro stated that he "would travel to Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates, that same day, for the signing of the contract". The document is signed by the deputy head of Desup, Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza, and cleared by the head, Belline Santana. The reader of the Petition 15.556 series knows both names: in March 2026 they would be the Central Bank officials under investigation for informal consultancy to Vorcaro. In November 2025 they were still signing letters about him.

Alberto Felix contended that he "never appeared as a Director or statutory member of Banco Master", that his signatures "arise from mere compliance with orders from superiors in the face of the need for a minimum quorum", that he was "a hired employee, with no privileged access, economic power or influence", and that he handed over "all passwords voluntarily". Ângelo Ribeiro said he had presented himself of his own accord to the Federal Police in São Paulo and that he cared for his elderly mother. Luiz Antonio Bull, 69, according to his habeas corpus, was not at home when the police arrived at 6:02 am and "returned immediately to present himself voluntarily"; he is responsible for a sister under legal guardianship. BRB's president, Paulo Henrique Costa, was abroad and brought his return forward to 19 November. Augusto Lima asked to be transferred to Brasília and reported a diagnosis of epilepsy.

Augusto Lima's argument is the one that most directly attacks the ruling, and he would develop it in February, by then at the Supreme Court, in a 26-page petition: he sold his Master shares to Vorcaro on 28 May 2024, with the transfer registered and a declaration from the bank; the facts under investigation would have begun at the end of 2024; he does not control the Bahian associations, one of which "was founded in 1970, that is, before the Applicant was born", and from the other he resigned the presidency in 2008; the mention of the associations would have been made "by Banco Master itself in a communication addressed to the Central Bank", without his knowledge; and the Central Bank authorised him, on 18 August 2025, to control another bank, Pleno, after the crime report had already been sent to the MPF. The judge, in his information to the TRF1, replied that Lima's activity "was not restricted to the formal corporate tie", that he appears as attorney-in-fact of one of the associations "with express powers to move funds", and that the Central Bank's diary records a meeting "on Saturday, 19 July 2025", with him present. Who is right, this text does not know. It knows that the dispute is over verifiable facts, dates and documents, and that that is what a case is decided on.

## The appeals court

The habeas corpus petitions were assigned to appellate judge Solange Salgado da Silva, of the TRF1. On 20 November she denied Bull's interim order, and the ruling, transcribed in the record, follows the judge's line: "the mere formal dissolution of the institution, removal from office and the freezing of assets are no guarantee that the criminal intent and the structure of the criminal organisation will not persist". The interim orders sought by Vorcaro, Augusto Lima and Ângelo Ribeiro were also denied that week, as the MPF records on 24 November.

On 28 November the same appellate judge revoked the detentions. The ruling itself is not among the filings this site has read; what is, and in three places, is its content: detention was replaced by periodic reporting, a ban on contact with suspects, witnesses and employees of Master and BRB, a ban on leaving the municipality and the country with the passport withheld, "suspension of the exercise of any activity of an economic or financial nature" and an ankle monitor. And by a warning, which the Federal Police would cite in March: "the breach of any of these conditions, the provision of false information, or the commission of any act that endangers public order may lead to the revocation of this benefit and the reinstatement of the detention order". Ten days between detention and release. A hundred days between release and the next detention, ordered by another judge, in another drawer, on the basis of facts which, according to the police, took place in that interval.

> Reading rule number fourteen: a revoked detention is not an annulled detention. The appeals court said that liberty with conditions was enough, not that the indications did not exist. Both things fit in the same ruling, and the press usually reports only one.
