Between Sunday's request and Wednesday's searches, a little under three days went by. Into them fits a dispute that is not about Vorcaro, but about who decides and in how much time. It is worth telling by the clock, because it was by the clock that it was fought.

## Monday, Tuesday

Monday, 2 March 2026. Justice André Mendonça issues an order: "let the file be sent to the federal prosecution service so that it may, as a matter of urgency, give its opinion within 24 hours on the requests made by the Federal Police, observing the strictly sealed nature of this proceeding". According to the Prosecutor General, the file reached his office at 3:36 pm.

Tuesday, 3 March, 4:01 pm. Paulo Gonet signs two pages. "The exiguous deadline, counted in hours, contemplated for the opinion of the prosecution service is impossible for the holder of the criminal action to meet." The record involves "ten individuals and five companies", "facts of high complexity" and "drastic measures, of the most intense interference with the most elementary fundamental rights". One does not see "any indication of imminent, immediate danger that would call for the extraordinary need for such a rapid and necessarily succinct analysis". He asks that "the measures await the opinion of the holder of the criminal action". And he warns: "before that analysis, the Prosecutor General's Office cannot be in favour of the precautionary requests and cannot endorse them".

At 6:54 pm, the police send a letter with "new, more likely addresses" for five targets, among them Vorcaro, in Nova Lima, and Paulo Sérgio, in Guaxupé, "without prejudice to" the earlier ones.

Tuesday night, the ruling, twenty pages. Item 8 records the justice's clock: "the file having been sent to the MPF on 2 March of the current year for 24 hours, the deadline lapsed *in albis* today, 03/03/2026, at 3:18 pm. With the deadline for its opinion already expired, the Prosecutor General's Office files a petition requesting an extension of the deadline". Item 9: "I deny the request for an extension, referring the statement of reasons for this to the ruling issued in Pet no. 15.556". The 15.556 ruling is the one on the detentions, decided the same night; the series on it tells the reasons. Here, the justice only adds that the record "gathers various items of evidence", among them "bank transfer receipts and electronic messages", which would attest to laundering, concealment, "continuation of the offences even after the start of the investigations" and "concrete threats of a violent nature against third parties opposed to Daniel Vorcaro".

## What the ruling says about each of them

The longest part of the ruling, from item 10.1 to 10.9, describes the targets one by one, "according to the police authority". It is, in good measure, the request rewritten in a judicial tone, and therefore milder. Vorcaro "would have taken part in defining strategies aimed at raising funds" and "maintained frequent dialogue with Central Bank officials". Zettel "would have acted directly in operational support", brokering payments, including "sums intended for the informal group called 'A Turma'". Mourão "played a central role in the operational organisation" of that group, with "searches and data extractions in restricted systems of public bodies using third parties' official credentials". Marilson "was a member of the informal group", using "professional experience and contacts acquired in his police career". Paulo Sérgio "provided strategic advice to the banker"; Belline, likewise, and "there are indications that he received a simulated contract proposal through the company Varajo". Palhares "signed a service proposal" and lent "an appearance of legitimacy to the contract". Ana Cláudia "acted in the operationalisation of financial transactions". The five companies are "typical shell companies".

On the boat captain, item 11 is clear: "although" the captain "is not a suspect", he "would himself have stated that he possesses and keeps evidence (footage/videos and telephone contacts)". Item 12 draws the line: "all, with the exception of" the captain, "took part in the offences". The search of his home is granted "for the exclusive purpose" of finding the videos.

The legal reasoning takes up six pages and is standard: article 240 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CPP) transcribed, fumus and periculum, proportionality. Two sentences say more. The first: the indications "show that there is a high probability that the targets of the search-and-seizure request are part of a broad, stable and well-structured money-laundering scheme created to perpetrate the diversion of sums in the billions, with the use of a militia responsible for making violent threats against third parties who go against the wishes of Daniel Vorcaro". The second, on method: "the simultaneous execution of the searches is appropriate in order to prevent the passing on of information and the prior manipulation of data".

The operative part grants the domiciliary and personal searches, authorises forced entry "through doors, safes, drawers, walls, cupboards", entry "at a different address" in case of a recent move or the "presence of a suspect whose detention has been ordered", access to cloud storage, e-mails and SIM cards, seizure of cash above R$ 20,000, jewellery, works of art, vehicles. At law offices, a Bar representative in attendance. And the usual warning: execution "in a calm, respectful and discreet manner, without any spectacle, as was correctly the case in the Federal Police's conduct on previous occasions". Once everything has been executed, "let the Prosecutor General's Office be notified", and "temporary" access to the file "for the lawyers admitted to the docket".

The same night, the justice's chambers send the police "19 (nineteen) search-and-seizure warrants".

## Wednesday, before dawn

Wednesday, 4 March, 4:46 am. A letter from the inquiries coordination unit: Mourão "slept at an address other than the one previously indicated", in a flat in Belo Horizonte, "so that the measure will be executed at the address below". At 5:04 am, another: Paulo Sérgio also slept somewhere else, a flat in São Paulo. Both invoke letter "c" of item 32 of the ruling. The justice acknowledges them the same day. How the police knew where each of them slept, the letters do not explain.

Thursday, 5 March, 12:02 pm. The police chief reports the result. Executed, fourteen warrants: Vorcaro in São Paulo and in Nova Lima; Paulo Sérgio in São Paulo and in Guaxupé; Belline, Palhares, Moriah, Zettel and Ana Cláudia in São Paulo; Zettel in Nova Lima; Mourão, Marilson and Ana Cláudia in Belo Horizonte; the captain in Ubatuba. Not executed, five, "considering that some companies had no real operations, and that some targets no longer lived at the addresses": Varajo and Super in São Paulo, the two Kings in Belo Horizonte, the captain in Florianópolis.

Let the sentence be noted. Of the five companies the police called a "laundering structure" and the justice called "shell companies", four had no address one could enter. That both confirms the thesis, a front has no office, and empties it: there is nothing to seize at a company that does not physically exist.

## Friday: six pages

Friday, 6 March, 4:59 pm. The Prosecutor General "acknowledges the ruling" and "clarifies", in seventeen numbered items, why he disagrees. He begins with the separation between investigating, prosecuting and judging, "an indispensable measure for the effectiveness of the Judiciary's impartiality". He recalls that the Court's internal rules only allow the referral to the Prosecutor General to be dispensed with "when there is urgency" and that "there was, moreover, neither indication nor evidence of the need for the measures to take place within a matter of hours".

Then, his chronology. There were three petitions, 15.556, 15.563 and 15.562. The first, sent on Friday, 27 February, "had its file made available to the Prosecutor General's Office on Saturday, at 11:18 am", with a 72-hour deadline. The other two were signed on Monday, 2 March, received "on the same day, at 3:36 pm", with 24 hours. "Deadlines begin to run from the first business day after service." The opinion went out "at 4:01 pm on 3 March". "Even if one contemplated (which is not the case) a count that excluded the day of service and counted the deadline in hours from the following hour", the opinion "was not made outside the deadline set by the reporting justice". And further: "the deadline set by the reporting justice in this case is not specifically established by law. The deadline arose from the reporting justice's impression of the time that seemed ideal to him".

Items 12 to 14 are the gravest. The prosecution service "cannot disregard the magnitude" of its mission; "the Prosecutor General does not have the option of being rash in his opinions, above all in those with the greatest potential impact on fundamental rights, as criminal measures are, detention among them". And: "the impact of certain precautionary criminal measures on fundamental values can be exemplified by the funeral event that occurred during the operation carried out". The sentence carries no name and no explanation. In the 15.562 record there is nothing more about it. The reader who follows this house knows which death, occurring in the days after the operation, the Prosecutor General is probably referring to; this record does not register it, and for that reason it is not named here.

Item 15 gives the measure of the work: "each of the three PETs sent to the Prosecutor General's Office had, each one of them, more than 700 pages. They therefore added up to more than 2,100 pages", on an inquiry "which has more than twenty thousand pages". And item 17 answers the justice's sentence that "the evidence of the offences and the urgency of adopting the measures requested are amply revealed in the Federal Police's request": "hearing the exclusive holder of the criminal action does not amount to, nor can it be considered, a formality devoid of real importance. If it were enough for the police request to refer to facts for it to be binding, there would be no need for an opinion from the prosecution service, nor even for a reasoned decision by the judge."

It ends with case law on contemporaneity and abstract gravity. One of the precedents cited was reported by André Mendonça himself.

The justice did not reply in this record. The searches had already been carried out. The Prosecutor General's Office, in the filings that follow, "acknowledges" each ruling and, when consulted, gives its opinion on returns of property and mirror copies. The discussion about the 24 hours stayed there, in six pages that nobody contested and that changed nothing.

> Reading rule number thirty-eight: when two clocks disagree, do not ask what time it is; ask who set them. The justice's read 3:18 pm and the Prosecutor General's 4:01 pm, and both were right, because they were counting different things. What was at stake was not the hour. It was whether, in a proceeding with three petitions and 2,100 pages, the prosecution service is there to give an opinion or to be informed. The answer, in this case, was given by the order of events.
