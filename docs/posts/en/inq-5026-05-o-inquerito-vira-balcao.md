There is a moment when an inquiry stops being an investigation and becomes an address: everyone who has something to say, or to ask for, writes there. Inquiry 5.026 reached that point in February 2026, when it changed reporting justice. This chapter, the last of the series, takes stock of what came through the door in the six months that followed: 23 decisions and orders by Justice André Mendonça, from 19 February to 28 August. Almost none of it is about BRB. Almost all of it is about who has to speak, where, and with what rights.

## The Senate's invitation

The new reporting justice's first decision, on 19 February, answers a request from the Senate's Economic Affairs Committee (CAE), signed by its chairman, Senator Renan Calheiros: authorisation for Daniel Vorcaro, then in detention, to give testimony before the committee on 24 February. The CAE had approved a motion to hear Vorcaro and Augusto Lima, and asked that the transport could be paid for "by the invitee himself".

The justice authorised it, with one condition and one prohibition. The condition: attendance would be "strictly optional and dependent on his explicit consent", because this was not a summons from a congressional inquiry committee, "but a request addressed by the Senate's Economic Affairs Committee to the suspect in the form of an invitation", and because the right against self-incrimination, since the ADPF rulings 395 and 444, "includes the choice of whether or not to attend the proceeding". The prohibition: transport would be "on an aircraft of the institution itself or on a scheduled commercial flight", under escort, "travel on any private aircraft being forbidden". A detainee offering to pay for the trip, and a justice saying: not on your plane. The CAE asked again for 10 March, and the justice extended the same decision.

## The formula

What follows, between 26 February and 27 March, is a sequence of almost identical decisions, and the repetition is the data. The congressional inquiry committee on organised crime (CPMI), created by Motion 470 of 2025 to "investigate the activity, expansion and functioning of criminal organisations on Brazilian territory, in particular gangs and militias", approved motions to summon people linked to the Master case. The summoned came to the Supreme Court to ask not to be forced to go. The justice gave all of them the same answer, in the same words: he recognises "the superlative importance" of the committee, recalls that "there is no obligation to attend nor any sanction for failing to attend", cites the same habeas corpus of 2019 and the same writ of mandamus of 2006, and "transmutes" the obligation into a choice, with the right to silence, to a lawyer, to not swearing to tell the truth and to not suffering "physical or moral constraints". "This decision serves as a safe-conduct."

Those who received that formula, in order: on 26 February, José Eugênio Dias Toffoli and José Carlos Dias Toffoli, summoned by four motions and who claimed to have been called "in the condition of suspects"; on the same day, Paulo Humberto Barbosa, whose petition noted that the justification for his summons was "identical" to that of the two previous ones and that a second motion spoke of an "unlawful relationship" between him and other summoned persons; on 3 March, Vorcaro himself, whom the CPMI wanted on 4 March "with attendance being compulsory" and who preferred "to invoke, on his lawyers' advice, the right to silence"; on 27 March, the lawyer Leonardo Augusto Furtado Palhares, under alternative measures in place of detention since March, to whom is imputed, "in theory", according to his own petition, participation in a scheme "involving the payment of improper advantages to a public official linked to the Central Bank", and who invoked, besides silence, a lawyer's professional privilege. About the first two names, what the previous chapter said still holds: the record does not explain what the CPMI wanted from them.

A reader may find the formula automatic. It is, and that is what makes it defensible: the reporting justice applied to everyone exactly the same criterion, and the criterion belongs to the full bench of the court, not to him. Whether the case law is right to prevent a congressional inquiry from forcing someone to appear in order to stay silent is another discussion, and a serious one; but it is not a discussion about this inquiry.

## The former Central Bank governor

The exception to the formula, and the longest decision of the six months, concerns Roberto Campos Neto, governor of the Central Bank from 2019 to 2024, summoned by the CPMI for 3 March. He did not ask for a safe-conduct; he asked that the summons be declared unlawful, for "misuse of purpose" and "fishing expedition", or, in the alternative, converted into an invitation, "in exactly the terms applied to the current governor of the Central Bank of Brazil, Gabriel Galípolo".

The justice transcribed the pages justifying the creation of the CPMI, which speak of homicides, gangs, a "new cangaço" and militias, and then the summons motion, which points as its "main causal link" to the "recent collapse of Banco Master, an institution involved in a fraud scheme that may reach R$ 17 billion", says that "investigations revealed that Banco Master allegedly moved about R$ 2.8 billion in foreign-exchange operations for a company suspected of laundering money for the Primeiro Comando da Capital", and criticises resolutions of the summoned man's tenure, such as the one that "loosened the rules for creating credit fintechs". The conclusion: "on the information available up to the present moment, there is no indication whatsoever of the summoned individual's involvement in the facts investigated within the so-called Operation Compliance Zero", and "the only link" would be "his condition as former governor of the agency". That, for the justice, constitutes "misuse and hollowing-out of purpose". But, "seeking to provide a solution grounded" in "minimal intervention" in another branch of government, and "resting on the constitutional principle of equal treatment", he did not quash the summons: he converted it into an invitation, as in Galípolo's case.

Two notes. The R$ 2.8 billion in foreign exchange linked to the PCC appears in the record of this inquiry only once, in this transcription of a congressional motion; no police or prosecution filing that this site has read supports or refutes it. And the sentence "there is no indication whatsoever of involvement" of Campos Neto is the reporting justice's, based on "the information available up to the present moment": the only statement of that kind about any person in all the decisions of the series.

## Service counter

Once the season of congressional inquiries had passed, the inquiry becomes a place of small requests, which are the routine of a case with suspects wearing ankle monitors. On 24 April, André Seixas Maia, Tirreno's director, wants a mobile phone and a laptop back; the PGR objects, because there is no word that the forensic examination has finished; the justice orders the police to say, within ten days, whether they have already extracted what they needed. On 30 April, Alberto Felix, under electronic monitoring, reports his father's death and asks to attend the wake in Passos, Minas Gerais; the justice grants it the same day, "on a strictly exceptional, temporary and conditional basis", invoking the article of the Penal Execution Act that allows provisional detainees to leave "upon the death of an ascendant", with return by 4 May and a ban on contact with any suspect. On 12 June, the same Alberto Felix needs authorisation to travel to Brasília to give a statement at the Federal Police's Special Inquiries Division, and the justice, in granting it, extends the same authorisation of his own motion to "the other suspects under personal measures restricting their liberty". On 28 August, a suspect named João Pedro Leite Nunes asks for access to the record and the postponement of his interview of 27 August; the justice notes that access had been granted on 24 August and that the date has already passed, and declares the requests "moot". On the same day, he sends the file to the PGR for comment on a request from the Federal District, BRB's majority shareholder, whose content the record does not reveal.

There is also COAF, the financial intelligence unit, on 26 February, asking how it should send its financial intelligence reports, since it had shared two in compliance with a decision in constitutional complaint 88.121, one spontaneously and the other "following a request from the CPMI on the INSS". The answer: the investigation "must follow the ordinary flow", and the procedure "must be the same as that ordinarily adopted" by COAF. No special regime for the Master case.

## The side doors

The most revealing order is dated 6 July: four petitions the inquiry did not ask for. Congresswoman Luciene Cavalcante asks for the inclusion of Congressman Mario Frias as a suspect, on the basis of an audio recording published by a news website on 19 May 2026, in which he "allegedly thanked Daniel Vorcaro for the funding granted to a film about the former President of the Republic". The same congresswoman asks for the inclusion of the mayor of São Paulo, Ricardo Nunes, on the "suspicion raised" of a connection between Banco Master, the concessionaire of the capital's funeral services and the city government that granted the concession in 2023. Senator Eudócia Caldas presents a criminal complaint (*notícia-crime*) asking for the reopening of closed inquiries on grounds of "thematic, evidentiary and institutional connection". And the Central Bank reports having identified irregularities in a second conglomerate, led by Entrepay, which includes Octa Sociedade de Crédito Direto: "trading of securities without sufficient backing", "misleading the BCB", "accounting artifices" and "diversion of funds" from payments, facts which, according to the Central Bank itself, "are connected to the unlawful acts being investigated within Operation Compliance Zero".

The justice neither included nor excluded anyone. He acknowledged that the petitions "have full correlation with the facts", but "display manifest thematic autonomy", and ordered each one detached as a separate petition, "in connection with the present Inquiry", "for specific consideration". That is how the numbered petitions that populate this archive are born: not by a decision to investigate someone, but by a decision not to mix things. Whoever wants to know what happened to the congressman, the mayor and the fintech must look in the petitions; if they find nothing, it is because the decision to investigate was not taken, or was not published.

## Where the series ends

On 25 August 2026, the Federal Police asked for more time "for the performance of complementary steps it deems indispensable for the completion of the investigations", and the justice granted "a further 60 days". It is the reporting justice's last word in the published record. The inquiry born of a Central Bank letter in March 2025 reaches September 2026 with no indictment, no dismissal, with five people who were detained and released and then detained again in another proceeding, with a public bank audited, a private bank liquidated, a consultancy that never made a single Pix transfer, and a central question that remains the same as on the first page: where did the loans that BRB bought come from. All the site could do was read the 26,694 pages and recount, in order, what they say and what they do not say. This last chapter is above all about the second thing.

> Reading rule number sixteen: when the reporting justice says "detach it" (*desentranhe-se*), he is saying "not now". A detached request has been neither denied nor accepted. It has been put in a drawer with a number, and the drawer can stay closed for a long time.
