There are two ways of reading Petition 15.562. The first is as a search-and-seizure proceeding, which is what it is: 148 filings and 1,209 pages, among which a twenty-page ruling sends the Federal Police into fourteen addresses on the morning of 4 March 2026. The second is as the place where the Banco Master investigation changed subject. Until February, the case was about loan portfolios, funds with a single quotaholder and a state-owned bank that bought rotten paper. From the filing that opens this docket onwards, the case is also about paid police officers, sealed systems consulted with someone else's login, a forged official letter sent to Facebook and seven men at a marina.

This series takes care of the second reading without forgetting the first. And it begins, as always, with filing number 1.

## Four signatures

The request runs to 159 pages and was signed in Brasília on the night of Sunday, 1 March 2026, by four police chiefs of the Federal Police's coordination unit for inquiries before the superior courts. It asks for "precautionary measures of search and seizure, personal and domiciliary". It comes with eight annexes: three police intelligence reports, the TRF1 ruling that released Vorcaro in November, a Central Bank document about a meeting of 17 November, a proof of an asset freeze, a Central Bank letter and the report on the search at the bank's headquarters.

The table of contents already tells the story. Section 2.1 deals with the "acts of corruption of Central Bank officials", which readers of this house know from the series on Petition 15.504: Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza, Belline Santana, the Varajo contract, Disney, the country estate. Section 2.2 is the novelty: "acts of obstruction of the investigation into a criminal organisation: violent intimidation, coercion, illegal telephone and telematic monitoring". Within it, five subsections: unlawful obtaining of sealed information; violence and intimidation "on the orders of Daniel Vorcaro"; illegal monitoring; the payments and the "financial operators" Fabiano Zettel and Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva; digital influencers; and, finally, the "compromise of the secrecy of the 1st phase", that is, how the banker learned he was going to be arrested.

## The police's summary of themselves

The first pages recapitulate. Banco Master, "liquidated by the Central Bank on 18/11/2025", had Daniel Vorcaro as its "director and principal manager". The business model: raising money through CDBs "with returns far above those offered by other financial institutions (120% of the CDI or higher)" and investing it "in high-risk funds and low-liquidity assets". When institutional funding failed, the bank went looking for money in public-sector pension schemes, "such as Rioprevidência and Amapá Previdência", and at BRB, which "injected around 12 billion reais into Master" by buying payroll-loan portfolios "that turned out, at least in good part, to be non-existent or in default". That is phase I, launched on 18 November 2025 by the 10th Federal Court of Brasília and later ratified at the Supreme Court. Phase II, on 14 January 2026, authorised by Justice Dias Toffoli, went after what the bank did with the money: receivables funds in which Master itself was the sole quotaholder and, at the far end, "'rotten' assets" issued by companies "indirectly related to relatives of Daniel Vorcaro".

Then comes the sentence that changes the subject. "With the still partial analysis of the material seized in Phase I, it was possible to establish that Daniel Vorcaro structured a veritable criminal organisation, aimed at shielding the unlawful acts committed in the management of Banco Master, including through the use of an armed wing of the criminal group, specialised in acts of intimidation, coercion and the obtaining of sealed data, which is done through the co-opting and corruption of police officers, federal ones included."

The material is, above all, the mobile phone seized from the banker in November. From it come the three police intelligence reports annexed: one on the Central Bank officials, and two, dated 19 and 26 February 2026, on the rest. The one of 26 February has 198 pages and a single signature; the one of 19 February, 126 pages and four.

## The four fronts

Page 7 organises what the police say they saw across "various state and parastatal vectors". At the Central Bank, "direct and recurrent dialogue" with the two heads of supervision, "who gave guidance, reviewed drafts addressed to the BCB and the FGC and interceded on matters of interest to the conglomerate", with payments made "through fictitious contracts and third-party companies". In parallel, a "service relationship" with Luiz Phillipi Machado de Moraes Mourão, "nicknamed 'Sicário', hereinafter referred to as Felipe Mourão, with a monthly remuneration of R$ 1,000,000.00", paid by companies in the names of Zettel and Ana Cláudia. Through him, "improper access" to systems of the federal prosecution service, of Senasp and of the Federal Police itself, "making use of third parties' official credentials". Official letters and institutional e-mails used to trigger the "law enforcement" channels of platforms such as Meta, "simulating official requests to take down profiles and obtain data". And a "vector of coercion and intimidation": orders to "go after" people he had fallen out with, "with express reference to the use of police officers or militias".

The conclusion, on page 150, completes the picture: "coordinated action on four fronts (influence over BCB officials, irregular obtaining of sealed information, coercion/threats against opponents and manipulation of media and social networks) made possible by financial operators and by the improper use of institutional credentials and channels". The list of crimes the police say they can see goes beyond Law 7.492: kidnapping and false imprisonment, threats, use of forged documents, usurpation of public office, unlawful access to a computer device, passive and active bribery, money laundering. And the measure of the damage, on the same page: "the largest banking fraud in Brazilian history", with a loss "to the deposit-guarantee fund alone of approximately 52 billion reais".

It is worth saying what that sentence is and what it is not. It is the police's estimate, with no calculation shown in this record. It is not a figure from the FGC or from the Central Bank. In the earlier series, the same loss appeared as R$ 12 billion at BRB and "around 12 billion" in uncovered CDBs, in the TRF1 ruling annexed here. The numbers grow according to who writes them and what they count.

## Who is on the list

The request, on pages 153 and 158, is for search and seizure at fourteen addresses. Nine individuals: Vorcaro and Zettel, "already targets of search-and-seizure warrants in earlier phases"; Mourão and Marilson Roseno da Silva, "retired federal police officer", who "are members of the armed wing"; Paulo Sérgio and Belline, "who would be receiving improper advantages"; Leonardo Palhares, "identified as the person responsible for disguising the improper advantage"; Ana Cláudia, "financially responsible for the illicit payments". Five companies: Moriah, Super, the two Kings (Participações and Motors) and Varajo. The police note that Palhares and Zettel "are enrolled with the Brazilian Bar Association" and that their professional safeguards will be respected.

The fourteenth name belongs to someone the police themselves say they are not investigating: the former captain of one of Vorcaro's boats, who, according to a recording described in the report of 26 February, said he had been threatened with death "by 07 militia members at the marina on the orders of Daniel Vorcaro" and had "footage" of the episode. The search of his home is requested "for the exclusive purpose of locating and seizing the videos, telephone contacts and other information leading to the full identification of the members of the 'Turma'". Because, the request admits, "so far we have the identification of the retired federal police officer Marilson Roseno, but at least 5 other members of this armed nucleus remain to be identified". We will come back to him, unnamed, in chapter 3.

The request recalls, on page 154, that "for the reasons set out in other petitions sent to this Supreme Court, warrants of pre-trial detention were requested". The detentions run in Petition 15.556, which has its own series. Here only the searches come in. But the argument is the same: "the criminal scheme is still in progress", and, without entering the homes, "there is a serious risk of concealment and/or alteration of evidence", because the suspects "have access to privileged information within the criminal-prosecution bodies".

## What the justice read

Justice André Mendonça summarised the filing in two paragraphs, on 3 March. The investigation would indicate that the group had "(i) corrupted Central Bank officials to obtain privileged information and regulatory interference; (ii) used financial operators and intermediary companies to conceal illicit payments; (iii) improperly accessed sealed systems of national public bodies, such as the federal prosecution service and the Federal Police, and international ones such as Interpol; (iv) employed mechanisms of intimidation, illegal monitoring and informational manipulation against critics and journalists". And the loss, in his reading, is "estimated at more than billions of reais", without the 52.

That is the frame. The following chapters step into it: first the "Turma" and the systems, then the threats, then the eve of 18 November, then the 24 hours given to the Prosecutor General's Office and, finally, what was left in the sealed boxes. Everything read in this series is what the police say they found on a telephone, what the justice decided on that basis and what the suspects, when they spoke, answered. Nobody had been charged over these facts as of September 2026.

> Reading rule number thirty-four: when an investigation changes subject, look at the date. Fraud was the theme until 18 November; the "armed wing" is born of the phone seized that day. What the police call a criminal organisation was found, not sought. That does not make it false. It makes it dependent on a single device, and the owner's account is not yet in this record.
