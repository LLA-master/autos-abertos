The Central Bank has a department whose name nobody outside the market knows and whose work everybody takes for granted: Desup, the bank-supervision department. It is the one that looks at banks from the inside. According to the ruling of 3 March, the head of that department and the deputy head chatted on WhatsApp with the owner of the bank they supervised, reviewed the letters the bank sent to the department itself, and were paid for it. This chapter is about that part of the ruling, the one the justice calls the "institutional corruption cell". It is the least violent part and, for anyone who understands the country, the gravest.

The same warning as in the earlier chapters applies: what follows is what the Federal Police presented and what the reporting justice found sufficient indication for precautionary measures. Nobody here has been tried. The Central Bank, the PF reports, had already removed the two officials from their positions of trust before the ruling.

## Congratulations

Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza was, "at the time of the facts", Deputy Head of Desup. The ruling says he kept "direct and frequent contact" with Daniel Vorcaro and "came to act informally in favour of the interests of the financial institution subject to supervision by the very agency he served". The first scene transcribed is small and says it all. Paulo Sérgio sends Vorcaro the image of the order appointing him to the post of deputy head. Vorcaro replies: "Congratulations".

From there the ruling describes a consultancy. Paulo Sérgio allegedly gave "suggestions to DANIEL VORCARO on how he should behave in a meeting with the President of the BACEN". He allegedly reviewed "drafts of documents and institutional communications prepared by Banco Master and addressed to the Central Bank itself", including a letter addressed to the department he himself helped to run, replying "with several suggested changes". He allegedly warned "the controller of Banco Master in advance about financial transactions that had been flagged by the agency's monitoring systems". He allegedly mentioned "potential parties interested in acquiring a financial institution linked to the conglomerate". The justice sums it up with an expression that is not a judge's but a columnist's: Paulo Sérgio "becomes a kind of employee/consultant of VORCARO for matters of exclusively private interest to the latter".

In the middle of all this there is a trip. Paulo Sérgio lets it be known, by message, that he was going to the Orlando theme parks, Disney and Universal. Vorcaro remarks that he would need to "arrange a guide for these people" and, says the ruling, "calls on a specific person to provide the service in question". The justice lists the episode as "another strong indication" of corruption. It is not the amount that impresses. It is the intimacy.

## The phone call instead of the message

Belline Santana was the Head of Desup. The ruling describes "the same kind of relationship" with him. Strategic advice on Master's regulatory situation, guidance on administrative proceedings, prior review of letters. He too was "urged by VORCARO to give an opinion on a letter that Banco Master would send to the Department he himself headed". Two details set him apart. First: "on several occasions, the suspect requested telephone contact to deal with sensitive matters, indicating an intention to avoid a written record of the communications". Second: he allegedly took part in "private meetings with DANIEL BUENO VORCARO, including outside the Central Bank's institutional premises".

The three of them, Vorcaro, Paulo Sérgio and Belline, allegedly belonged to a message group "created to facilitate direct communication between those involved", in which "documents, information and requests for support addressed to the Central Bank officials were shared, and the officials frequently showed themselves available to analyse and comment on the content forwarded". A WhatsApp group between the supervised and his supervisors. If there were a manual of what not to do, it would be the cover.

## The consultancy for young people

How does one pay a Central Bank official without it looking like payment? The record's answer is called Varajo Consultoria Empresarial Sociedade Unipessoal Ltda. According to the ruling, Belline "received a simulated engagement proposal" through that company, "structured for the purpose of justifying payments related to the informal services rendered to the controller of Banco Master". The object of the contract, as described in item 71, is a small masterpiece of the genre: participation in a "supposed project to prepare a technical study on bringing young people into the financial market".

The proposal was signed for Varajo by its administrator, Leonardo Augusto Furtado Palhares. The ruling attributes exactly that to him: signing and formalising the document, "lending the engagement an appearance of legitimacy". The terms, according to the record, were discussed by Fabiano Zettel, the same financial operator from the previous chapter. And the Federal Police, transcribed, says Varajo's accounts served as a "pass-through account" for the receipts.

The transcribed messages about the payment are short. Zettel to Vorcaro: "Today we have to pay Belline's first one, ok?". Vorcaro: "OK". Then Vorcaro insists that the payment be made by someone else, identified as Palhares: "But see whether Leo can pay. And we reimburse the next day. Quite important, this." Zettel: "Actually I was going to pay Léo and Léo pay him. But I'll ask him to pay first". On another occasion: "Belline chasing. Pay?". Answer: "Of course". And there is, says item 70, an explanation from Ana Cláudia to Vorcaro "of how they arrange for the payment not to go directly from VORCARO to BELLINE", which the justice calls "a detailed description of the money laundering".

The Federal Police sums up the mechanics: the payments were made "by FABIANO CAMPOS ZETTEL and ANA CLAUDIA QUEIROZ DE PAIVA, on the orders of DANIEL VORCARO, mainly, but not exclusively, through the company SUPER PARTICIPAÇÕES E EMPREENDIMENTOS S.A.". The same structure, says the PF, that paid the crew. The reader coming from chapter 2 recognises the design: a box (Super), an operator (Zettel), an executor (Ana Cláudia), and different destinations according to the service.

## What the justice decided for them

For the two officials, the PF did not ask for detention. It asked for, and the ruling granted, what the Code of Criminal Procedure calls alternative measures (*medidas cautelares diversas*): a ban on contact with witnesses and the other suspects; a ban on "frequenting or even entering the Central Bank's premises"; a ban on leaving the municipality and the country, with surrender of passport within 48 hours; suspension from public office; and electronic monitoring by ankle monitor. Palhares and Ana Cláudia received the same package, minus the Central Bank part, because they are not civil servants.

The PF's justification, which the justice adopts, is institutional: the two "hold positions directly related to the supervision of financial institutions" and "have access to strategic and confidential information"; their remaining in office "creates an environment conducive to the continuation of the unlawful conduct", including obstruction, "which may compromise the credibility of the regulatory body". The justice adds that, for these four, "pre-trial detention, at this moment, can be replaced by less severe measures", with one clause: "without prejudice to future reassessment in the event of non-compliance".

The ankle-monitor rules take up two pages and are of a bureaucratic minuteness that, read in sequence, has something of a comedy of manners about it: keep a minimum distance of fifty metres from the other suspects; recharge the device "as instructed by the monitoring centre"; report any change of address within the municipality and ask for judicial authorisation to change municipality; do not enter "the corporate headquarters or offices of the companies under investigation". The monthly reports go to the PF team in Brasília, which informs the court only of "significant and repeated non-compliance".

There is also an act that leaves the criminal sphere. In item 121, the justice authorises sharing with the Central Bank the evidence against the two officials "for disciplinary investigation", and orders the agency to report what it has done within thirty days and every six months thereafter. In July, the federal comptroller (CGU) would ask for and obtain the same sharing, for an asset inquiry and possible liability proceedings, naming the two officials and the five companies. Corruption, when it exists, is tried in three courts: the criminal one, the administrative one and that of one's career. The March ruling opened all three.

## The five companies

The chapter closes with the legal entities, because the ruling closes with them. Five companies were suspended for an indefinite period: Varajo Consultoria, Moriah Asset Empreendimentos e Participações, Super Empreendimentos e Participações, King Participações Imobiliárias and King Motors Locação de Veículos e Participações. The ruling says they were "run by members of the criminal structure" and lists the five uses the police attribute to them: fictitious consultancy contracts, investments linked to the group, an intermediary structure for moving funds, simulated asset transactions, payment of the expenses of the unlawful activities.

To reach the suspension, the justice delivers a short speech on free enterprise, which is a foundation of the Republic, and on the social function of property. He says that a company that commits "an isolated unlawful act" deserves a reprimand, not a shutdown. But that the reasoning changes "when a legal entity is created not to produce wealth and generate jobs, but to act exclusively in the commission of unlawful acts". In this case, he writes, "there being no indication of any element pointing to the actual performance of lawful economic activities". It is a strong sentence about five company registrations (CNPJs). Moriah Asset, it should be noted, appears in this site's records in other proceedings, with dozens of filings; whether or not it had lawful activity is a question the ruling answers on a summary assessment, and one the record that follows will have to answer for real.

> Reading rule number four: corruption, in the record, rarely appears as an envelope. It appears as a consultancy, as a technical study, as "see whether Leo can pay". What one reads is the form; what has to be proved is the substance.
