Every large Brazilian investigation is born twice: when it happens and when it turns into paper. The second birth is the bigger one. On 11 September 2026 the Supreme Federal Court published the record of Petition 15.556 and of fourteen other proceedings of Operation Compliance Zero, the Banco Master case. A compressed archive of 23.8 gigabytes. Inside it, 4,258 PDF documents and 49 videos. Added together, 189,371 pages.

Do the arithmetic nobody does. At a heroic pace of two hundred pages a day, no Sundays and no holidays, a reader would finish in two years and seven months. He would then be ready to start understanding. That is why nobody reads. People read the summary of the summary, and the summary of the summary has an author, and the author has an interest. This site exists to shorten that chain without replacing it with another.

## What is inside

The archive is not a book: it is a cupboard. And the cupboard lies about proportion. Of the fifteen proceedings, a single one, Petition 15.198, takes up 122,857 pages, almost two thirds of the total. Not because much is decided in it, but because much was attached to it: copies of the records of federal cases, DVD media turned into PDF, evidence piled on evidence. The largest individual file in the whole package has 12,373 pages and is the full copy of a criminal action pending before the 6th Federal Criminal Court of São Paulo. An entire criminal case inside a petition, like a court clerk's Russian doll.

Now the proportion that matters. Sort the filings by what they are. "Supporting documents", that is, attachments: 1,202 files, 146,107 pages, 77% of the archive. Petitions from lawyers and from the prosecution service: 871 files, 5,909 pages. Electronic filing receipts, the proof that a petition was lodged: 782 files. There are almost as many receipts as petitions, which is bureaucracy in its purest state. Powers of attorney: 303. Identity documents: 57, and of those the site reads not a single line, by decision.

And the rulings? The single-justice rulings of the reporting justice, Justice André Mendonça, add up to 68 documents in 754 pages. With the procedural orders, 198 documents and 1,039 pages. Half a percent of the archive. Everything the court has actually decided in this case, so far, fits inside a Russian novel. The rest is what was put together to persuade it.

> Reading rule number one: volume is not importance. The filing that changes a case may run to five pages. The twelve-thousand-page attachment may be a bank statement.

## What there is in the way of people

The text was combed through. The count came to 14,875 distinct CPFs and 8,127 distinct CNPJs in the 189 thousand pages. Almost fifteen thousand individuals with a taxpayer number. Most are not under investigation, are not parties, are not anything: they are the holder of an account on a statement, a witness, the neighbour named in a power of attorney. That is why the site adopted a simple and unfriendly rule: a person's name only gets in if it appeared in a ruling, a procedural order or an initiating petition, or if it reappears in different proceedings. The others become a code, "Person A1B2C3" (*Pessoa A1B2C3*), and the code lets you see that someone links two dots without saying who. Of the 716 names left in the database, 357 are named and 359 are codes. Almost half and half. The archive is public; the lives of those who passed through it by chance are not.

## Which period is being talked about

The documents cite dates, and the dates tell a story the cover does not. In the narrative filings, petitions, rulings, police requests, the most cited month is October 2025, with nineteen thousand mentions, followed by November and December of the same year. That is the quarter the case talks about most. It is not necessarily when things happened: it is when the record discusses them. The difference between the two is the difference between a fact and a proceeding, and whoever confuses the two is writing an editorial.

## What to do with this

A site does not read for you. What it does is say where each thing is: every profile comes with a table called "Where to check", with the proceeding, the filing number and the page. Download the STF package, open the folder, go to the page. If what is there contradicts what is here, the site is wrong, and the correction is public.

One hundred and eighty-nine thousand pages. Nobody will read it all. But, for the first time, anyone can read the right page.
