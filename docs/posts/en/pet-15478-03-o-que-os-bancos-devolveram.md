After the ruling of 22 June 2026, Petition 15.478 begins to receive replies. They are the rest of the proceeding, and they are, for anyone expecting revelations, an instructive disappointment: the public record registers that the data were sent, how many rows they have, and where they went. The content is not here. This chapter is about what can be learnt by looking only at the envelopes.

## Seventy-eight

The first to reply was the Central Bank, on 10 July. The Supreme Court's letter had reached the authority on 26 June, and on 29 June it had relayed the order to every institution with which the thirteen names hold or have held a relationship, according to the financial system's customer register. The list takes up a page and a half and the site read it in full: 78 institutions. There are the big banks, the brokerages, the credit cooperatives, half a dozen payment institutions and fintechs, and a handful of names the reader of the other series will recognise: "BRB, Banco de Brasília S.A."; "Banco Master S.A., in Liquidation" (*em Liquidação Extrajudicial*); "Master S.A. Corretora, in Liquidation"; "Banco Pleno S.A., in Liquidation"; "Cartos Sociedade de Crédito Direto S.A."; "Trustee Distribuidora de Títulos e Valores Mobiliários"; a distributor called Sefer, also "in Liquidation"; and Maree, a payment institution which, as will be seen, belongs to a marketplace.

Three of those lines tell a story on their own. Banco Pleno, which Augusto Lima was authorised to take control of in August 2025 and which, in February 2026, his lawyers were presenting as proof of good standing before the Central Bank, appears in July 2026 as "in Liquidation". Trustee, a distributor investigated in Operation Quasar in São Paulo, whose director had on his phone conversations about "DV, ultimate beneficiary", is one of the institutions where one of the thirteen names holds an account. And Cartos, the credit company the police say is one and the same as Tirreno, is on the list as a depositary financial institution, which is the only way Tirreno could have had, after all, an account anywhere.

The Central Bank also confirmed that it had sent, through Simba, the customer-register data for "case 002-PF-012276-49". That is the number identifying this disclosure order in every document that follows.

## The rows

Itaú replied on 13 July with two transmission receipts. The first lists the files sent and the number of rows in each: branches, 27; accounts, 56; holders, 59; statement, 8,598; origin and destination, 8,598. Each statement row is one entry. Eight thousand six hundred entries, over two years, in the accounts the thirteen names hold at a single bank. Bradesco replied on 16 July with bigger numbers: 454 branches, 1,426 accounts, 2,042 holders, 36,099 statement rows, plus a five-megabyte file of "shares, investments and funds", one from its brokerage and one of foreign exchange. Bradesco's letter is the most informative of all, because it says what it found and what it did not find. It located investments in the name of ten of the thirteen: eight people, the Terra Firme of Bahia and Viking. It found no investments in the name of Ângelo Ribeiro, of Terra Firme Construções or of Tirreno. It located a "foreign exchange transaction in the name of Daniel Bueno Vorcaro" and securities transactions in the name of Augusto Lima and of Paulo Henrique Costa. And it "found no active accounts" for Terra Firme Construções or for Tirreno.

Maree, the payment institution of the Shopee group, replied on 16 July that "only Mr Daniel Bueno Vorcaro appears in our database", and transmitted what it had: a five-kilobyte statement and five documents with generic names, "foreign exchange contract", "copy of cheque", "credit card bill", "foreign exchange record". A banker under investigation over seventeen billion has, in a shopping app, an account like anyone else.

These numbers say nothing about the merits. They speak of scale: tens of thousands of entries, from a couple of dozen institutions, which the police will cross-check against the tax returns to "demonstrate the growth in wealth" and "identify all the persons who benefited". The public archive ends where the analysis begins.

## The tax authority

The reply from the tax authority (Receita Federal), dated 7 July, signed by its special secretary and supported by a technical note, is the most interesting document of the chapter because, in explaining what it sent, it says three things the record did not say.

The first is about the request. The police had asked for the "integrated dossier", a tool that gathered several of the tax authority's databases; the note informs that "the Integrated Dossier was discontinued at the beginning of 2025", because "some databases were discontinued and others ceased to be updated", and that it would send the individual returns in its place. They had asked for property transactions "since 1980"; the tax authority limited it to the period of the disclosure order, "with a view to preserving the taxpayers' tax secrecy". They had asked for a return for inactive legal entities that "was abolished in 2016" and another that "was in force until calendar year 2014". The police request, on this point, was an old form, and the tax authority corrected it with bureaucratic patience.

The second is about what does not exist. "The Return on Transactions Settled in Cash returned no data for the taxpayers in question." None of the thirteen declared, in 2024 or 2025, receiving or paying cash above the threshold. And: "there are no tax audit procedures on record against the taxpayers at issue, for the period of interest". The tax authority was auditing no one.

The third is the one that complicates the hypothesis of chapter 1. The note carries a table of the taxpayers who failed to file returns, and in it "Terra Firme Construções e Incorporações Ltda" appears with the remark: "DEREGISTERED (*baixada*) since 26 October 2018 by reason of extinction through closure, voluntary liquidation". The company the police listed as the recipient of "instalments above R$ 3 million" paid by Master in 2024 and 2025 has been, for the tax authority, extinct since 2018. Bradesco, as seen, found no active accounts for it. There are two possible explanations, and the record does not choose between them: the invoices the police saw on the phone were from another company of the same group, the "Terra Firme of Bahia", or the "Terra Firme SP" in the file name, and the police included the wrong CNPJ; or the payments were made to a deregistered legal entity, which would in itself be a problem. The site records the discrepancy and does not resolve it. The table also states that Daniel Vorcaro, Tirreno, Viking and the Terra Firme of Bahia had not filed their 2025 accounting books, "considering that the deadline has not yet closed".

## What remains

The São Paulo state tax authority received the letter on 26 June, at 2:34 pm, and opened it fifteen seconds later, according to the electronic receipt the Supreme Court's Registry added to the record, with the full trace of the connection. It is the last substantive document. There is no reply from it in the public filings, nor from the other 75 institutions besides Itaú, Bradesco and Maree. It may be that they replied directly through Simba, as the ruling allowed; it may be that they did not reply. The record does not say.

The balance of Petition 15.478 is, then, this. A request with the first readings of the phones, adopted by the PGR in three weeks and by the justice in four months. A list of thirteen names and a two-year sweep, authorised with the clause that everything may be shared with the sister petitions. And, in return, receipts: 78 institutions, tens of thousands of entries, none of them legible here. What the police find in those files will appear, if it appears, in another petition, in a financial analysis report, or in an indictment. Until then, the proceeding that authorised the looking is also the proceeding that most clearly shows that the public does not look: secrecy was lifted for the State, not for the reader.

There remains, on the reader's side, a question the series cannot answer and records for whoever can. The police said Terra Firme received more than three million a month from Master "precisely in the period of greatest crisis". The tax authority said the Terra Firme on the list has been deregistered since 2018. One of the two statements carries an identification error, and the difference between them is the difference between a diversion of money and a typing mistake. The public archive, in September 2026, does not allow one to know which.

> Reading rule number twenty-seven: when a public proceeding is about confidential data, what one reads are receipts. Read the receipts with the care of someone reading bank statements, because sometimes the receipt says the account does not exist.
