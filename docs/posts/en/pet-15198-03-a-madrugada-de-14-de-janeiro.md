Police operations have two versions: the next day's, with photographs of evidence bags, and the record's, with times. The record's is better. In Petition 15.198, the launch of the second phase of Operation Compliance Zero, on 14 January 2026, left a trail of three rulings and one procedural order which, read in sequence, tell a backstage story nobody reported: that of a reporting justice who thought the Federal Police was taking too long and said so in writing, in capital letters.

## 8 January: thirty-nine addresses

Two days after granting the secrecy lifts and the freezes, Justice Dias Toffoli received from the PGR the "supplementary request" for searches: a list of 39 individuals and companies, with addresses supplied by the police. The list is public in the record and this site does not reproduce it, because an address is not news. The names that matter for understanding the case have already appeared: Daniel Vorcaro, with three addresses between São Paulo and Belo Horizonte; his father, Henrique; his sister, Natália, and his brother-in-law, Fabiano Zettel; his brother, Felipe; Nelson Tanure; João Carlos Falbo Mansur; Maurício Quadrado; Ascendino Garcia, "Dino"; Fernando Alves Vieira; Clínica Mais Médicos and its president; the asset managers WNT and Acura; Sefer; Banco Master itself, at three addresses. One target, Benjamim Botelho, was listed as "impaired, as he does not reside in national territory".

Toffoli granted it "in part". He excluded three: Vorcaro's first address, target number 12 and Banco Master, because at those "searches of the same kind had previously been carried out". And he added an observation that says a great deal about the state of the bank in January: Master "is under extrajudicial liquidation, with an intervenor (*interventor*) appointed by the Central Bank", who already has "all the files and documents", so that "new on-site searches" were not needed. He ordered that the searches be carried out "as discreetly as possible, with press or third-party accompaniment forbidden, and with the privacy of the persons subject to the measure preserved". Discreet. Hold on to the word.

## 12 January: twenty-four hours

The public record does not contain the text of the order of 12 January, but the next day's ruling describes it: at 2:52 pm, the reporting justice signed an order that the phase be launched "within 24 hours". The police did not launch it. What the police did instead was to file, at 7:13 pm on 13 January, an urgent petition, and it is that petition that opens the window.

## 13 January, 7:13 pm: the flight

The Federal Police reported that, "while monitoring the suspects", it had found that Fabiano Campos Zettel "holds an international air ticket to Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates, booked for the early hours of 14 January 2026, a few hours before the execution of the judicial measures ordered in the present precautionary proceeding (00:50 hrs)". And that Nelson Tanure had a flight "for the morning of 14 January 2026, at 7:30 hrs, to Curitiba/PR", which could take him out of his home "before the time at which the police team is allowed to enter the residence", that is, before six in the morning, the hour from which the law allows entry into a home with a warrant.

The solution the police proposed was surgical and, in its way, elegant. For Zettel: a personal search "wherever he is found (which will probably be at his boarding at Guarulhos international airport, São Paulo)", seizure "especially [of] his mobile telephone", and temporary detention "for the minimum period of one day", because, once the other warrants had been executed, "the suspect's release may be ordered (from 6 am on 14 January 2026)". The detention was not meant to detain; it was meant to stop him, when approached at the airport at midnight, from warning the family before the police knocked on the other doors at six. The Federal Police said so in as many words: "the criminal core is made up of close relatives of his". Plus retention of his passport and a ban on leaving the country. For Tanure: a personal search at the Rio airport the next morning. The PGR agreed at 8:49 pm.

## 13 January, night: the justice

Toffoli's ruling, signed that same night, has eight pages and two temperatures. The first is the jurist's: he applies the requirements the Supreme Court itself set in ADIs 3.360 and 4.109 (direct actions of unconstitutionality) for temporary detention, concrete indispensability, indications of authorship in a crime on the statutory list, contemporaneity, suitability, and concludes that they "are met". He orders Zettel's temporary detention "until 07:00 on 14 January 2026", the personal search of both men, the seizure of the passport, the ban on leaving the country.

The second temperature is that of a reporting justice who felt disobeyed. "What astonishes this Reporting Justice (*causa espécie*) is not only the breach of the deadline I set", he writes, "but also the lack of commitment to complying with a judicial order for which the Federal Police had several days of planning and preparation". And, further on, in capitals that this text reproduces because they are in the original: "any frustration of the execution of the measures requested stems from the exclusive inertia of the FEDERAL POLICE, including in the face of EXPRESS AND DELIBERATE DISREGARD OF A DECISION ISSUED BY ME ON 12 JANUARY 2026". Any harm to the other measures would be "the entire responsibility of the police authority".

Two rare orders follow. First: "all goods and materials SEIZED" were to be "SEALED and HELD IN CUSTODY directly at the seat of the SUPREME FEDERAL COURT". Not at the Federal Police: at the Supreme Court. Second: the Director-General of the Federal Police would have 24 hours to "inform this Court of the reason for the non-compliance with the order". The ruling orders publication from noon on 14 January, when the doors would already have been knocked on.

The public record does not contain the Director-General's answer, and this text does not invent one. What it contains is what came the next day.

## 14 January: the success and the reconsideration

The operation happened. It was on that day, according to the other series, that the police froze R$ 2.245 billion in the account of Vorcaro's father at REAG. It was on that day that six cars left Henrique Vorcaro's garage in Belo Horizonte, and two Porsches that of Thiago Assumpção Henriques, and thirteen guns the house of Fernando Alves Vieira, all matters for chapter 5. Toffoli's procedural order, dated that same 14 January, opens with the words "in view of the success of the operation carried out today".

The order exists because the command to seal everything at the Supreme Court lasted less than a day. The police asked for reconsideration, pointing to "the risks of frustrating the operation if the seized devices were not immediately submitted to technical and forensic examination". The PGR agreed and asked for more: authorisation to carry out itself "the extraction and analysis of the entire body of evidence". Toffoli granted it to the PGR, "the recipient of the evidentiary material", so that it could have "a systemic view of the alleged crimes of great proportions", and ordered a letter to the Director-General of the Federal Police to forward the material "directly to the PGR". With an instruction of care that is almost domestic: "it being sufficient for that purpose that the devices be kept electrically charged and disconnected from telephone and wi-fi networks".

In one day, the seized material went from "sealed at the STF" to "extracted by the PGR", and the police, called inert the day before, were left out of the custody. A month later, with another reporting justice, the forensic work would go back to the Federal Police's ordinary flow. That is chapter 4.

## What this says

About Zettel, it says the police believed he was going to leave. He did not: he was approached, held until seven, handed over his passport and, in March, would be detained again, this time pre-trial, in Petition 15.556. About Tanure, it says only that he was going to Curitiba; the personal search was authorised, and nothing in the record of this petition says what was seized. About the relationship between the Supreme Court and the Federal Police at the height of the operation, it says what the next day's version did not: there was friction, it was put on the record, and the Court demanded an explanation.

If the explanation came, it is in a filing that is not a ruling and that this site has not read for you. Reading rule number three applies here too: what is transcribed exists in the record; what is not, does not exist for this text.

> Reading rule number nine: in an operation, the hour matters more than the day. Fifty past midnight, six in the morning, seven in the morning, noon: each of those hours is somebody's decision.
