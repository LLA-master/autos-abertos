Every organisation has a department nobody mentions at the results meeting. According to the ruling of 3 March, the one in the group under investigation was called "A Turma" (the crew), and the justice's description of it is the part of the document that strays furthest from the world of banking and comes closest to the world of ordinary crime: surveillance, collection of confidential data, intimidation of "business competitors, former employees and journalists". It is also the part where the ruling stops paraphrasing and starts transcribing WhatsApp messages, in quotation marks. This chapter follows the quotation marks. Where the ruling withholds names, this text withholds them too; and it recalls, as the document itself does, that all of this is what the police presented and what the judge found sufficient for a precautionary measure. It is not a verdict.

## The man on the bridge

The cell had, according to the record, a coordinator: Luiz Phillipi Machado de Moraes Mourão, identified in the communications as "Felipe Mourão" and, says the ruling, "going by the nickname 'Sicário', itself indicative of the nature of his activities". The sentence is the justice's. Mourão allegedly kept a "direct service relationship" with Daniel Vorcaro, and his role would have been "obtaining confidential information, monitoring people and neutralising situations considered sensitive".

The method described is that of someone who knows the systems from the inside. According to the police authority, Mourão ran "queries and data extractions in restricted systems of public bodies", using "official credentials belonging to third parties", and allegedly obtained improper access "to the systems of the Federal Police itself, of the federal prosecution service, and even of international bodies such as the FBI and Interpol". He would also look after the group's digital reputation: the ruling speaks of requests to remove content and profiles "that simulated official requests from public bodies", sent to service channels reserved for the authorities.

The Federal Police, transcribed in the ruling, sums up his role with an image: Mourão "is the bridge between DANIEL VORCARO's wishes to influence public opinion and the influencers hired by the criminal organisation". The bridge, in the record, had a price.

## The monthly

Item 34 says there are "strong indications" that Mourão received "a million a month from VORCARO through FABIANO ZETTEL". The evidence presented is a sequence of messages the justice transcribes. First the chasing: "Good morning. Fabiano didn't send this month and the crew is asking. Have a look with him please. Thanks." Then, asked about amounts, the explanation of how the money was shared out:

> "He sends the monthly and I split it among the crew. I send it to them. 400 split among 6. The boys I send 75 each, mine. The DCM and two more editors. That's the monthly. He sends 1 and when you send a bonus I split it among the boys and the crew."

Read it slowly, because it is an organisation chart. A million comes in. Four hundred thousand is split six ways. "The boys" (*os meninos*) get 75 each. There are "the DCM and two more editors" on the payroll. And there are bonuses. In a third message, an employee of Vorcaro's, Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva, asks: "Will it be 1 mm as usual?". Answer: "Yes". The ruling says she then made the transfer and attached the receipt for one million reais paid into the account indicated by Mourão, belonging to a company the ruling calls, in that passage, King Empreendimentos Imobiliários e Participações Ltda. Among the five companies the same document would order suspended is a King Participações Imobiliárias Ltda., which the police describe as Mourão's company; whether they are the same legal entity under different spellings, the record does not clarify in this filing.

The payer, according to the record, was Fabiano Campos Zettel, described by the police as the group's "financial operator" and Vorcaro's "brother-in-law". The ruling says Zettel "handled the operational side of payments to the informal group known as 'A Turma'" and that the structure was the same one used to pay public officials, "with the difference that, in this case, the money goes from the company SUPER PARTICIPAÇÕES to the companies of FELIPE MOURÃO". Super Empreendimentos e Participações S.A. appears in the record as the box from which the transfers left; Ana Cláudia, as a partner in it.

## What the crew did, according to the messages

The hardest part of the ruling is items 38 to 47, and there is no way to soften them without betraying the document. A former employee of Vorcaro's was allegedly being monitored; Mourão asks whether there is "a phone or something like that to monitor". An employee who had supposedly made an unwanted recording was to be intimidated; in the messages, says the ruling, his personal documents circulate, and Vorcaro orders them to "dig up everything on the two of them", the employee and a chef associated with him. The next sentence is transcribed in quotation marks: "The good thing about giving the chef a shake (*dar sacode*) first. The other one will get scared straight away."

There is a housemaid. Vorcaro writes that she was allegedly threatening him and uses an expression that the ruling reproduces and this text does not, because the person in question is not a public figure and the site does not publish her name. Mourão replies: "What needs doing?". The order: "Pull her address, everything".

And there is a journalist. The ruling says it withholds his name; this text does the same. Mourão remarks that the professional "clocks in every Sunday" and proposes "putting out a new one", a positive one, that is, a favourable article. Vorcaro: "Should have had people following this guy. To get everything on him." Mourão: "I'll do that." In another exchange, the message the justice singles out:

> "This lauro, I want to have him beaten up. Break all his teeth. In a mugging."

Mourão replies with two thumbs-up and writes that they are "on top of all the negative links", that they will "take them all down" and "put out positive ones". He asks: "Can I? I'll look into it...". The transcribed answer is "Yes". The justice concludes, in item 46, that there are "strong indications that VORCARO instructed MOURÃO to stage a robbery, or simulate a similar scenario, in order to violently harm the journalist in question and, from that episode, silence the voice of the press". And he generalises in item 47: "there are countless episodes along the same lines".

One must stop here and say what these paragraphs are. They are transcriptions made by the police of seized messages, selected by the police, presented to the judge in a request for detention and found by him to be strong indications. The defence had not been heard; in a precautionary measure, it is not. What can be stated is that these messages exist in the record and that the justice read them this way. What cannot be stated, and the ruling does not state, is that anyone has been convicted on account of them.

## The fourth man

Marilson Roseno da Silva is the only member of the crew with a State biography: a retired federal police officer, a police clerk, as one learns from a June ruling on his pension. According to the record, he belonged to the crew as "one of the main operators of this coercion cell", using "experience and contacts stemming from his police career" to obtain sensitive data and watch targets. The ruling places him "under the operational leadership" of Mourão, with the aim of "anticipating or neutralising risks arising from official investigations or from the activity of journalists, former employees and other individuals considered critical".

The PF, transcribed, calls the crew an "armed wing", a "private militia", and says the investigation would be at risk "including as to the physical integrity of the public servants responsible for the inquiry (PF, MPF, STF, BCB)". It is the rhetoric of a police request, and the ruling reproduces it as such, in quotation marks. What the justice takes on in his own name is in item 91: "there are strong indications of the existence of a group intended to intimidate adversaries and monitor the authorities, which reveals a concrete risk of interference in the investigations". That is why this chapter exists, and not because of the hole in the bank: the crew is the central argument for the detention. Without it, the case would be a banking case.

## What became of the crew

All four were detained. Mourão, at the moment of his arrest, was allegedly found with an undocumented firearm, which gave rise to a separate arrest in flagrante, on 4 March. On 6 March, three days after the ruling, Mourão died. The defence reported the death with a certificate; on 29 May the justice declared the State's power to punish him extinguished, which is what the law requires when a suspect dies, and kept the asset freezes in place, because repairing damage is not a penalty and passes to the heirs. Marilson, in detention, asked in June for his police clerk's pension to be released to support his family; the justice refused to unfreeze the accounts and allowed the benefit to be paid into his wife's account instead. The crew, as a structure, ends there in the record of this petition: one dead, one detained, a paymaster detained, an alleged principal detained, all awaiting a criminal case that, when the seal fell, had not yet begun.

> Reading rule number three: when the ruling transcribes a message, it is saying "this exists in the record", not "this happened". Between one and the other lies an entire trial.
