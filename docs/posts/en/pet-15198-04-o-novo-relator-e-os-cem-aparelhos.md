There is a procedural order in Petition 15.198 that has four pages, three of them blank. On the fourth, one line: "To the Initial Processing Coordination Office of the Judicial Secretariat, for the steps of redistribution." It is signed by the president of the Court, Edson Fachin, on 13 February 2026. Before it, every ruling in this petition is by Justice Dias Toffoli; after it, every one is by Justice André Mendonça. The public record does not say the reason for the change, and this text does not suppose one. It records the fact, because it changes the style, the pace and, on one point, the direction of the proceeding.

## 19 February: a hundred devices, twenty weeks

The new reporting justice's first ruling answers a letter from the Federal Police about the material seized on 14 January. The National Institute of Criminalistics had reported that "the forensic demand involves about 100 electronic devices, it being estimated that a single expert would need approximately 20 weeks of exclusive dedication to carry out the extraction examinations". The "most urgent" extractions had already been done "on an exceptional basis", at the PGR's request, "to prevent the loss of evidentiary content". The Federal Police asked for two things: that the rest follow "the Institution's ordinary flow of forensic work", and that the goods remain "in the Federal Police's depositories, as ordinarily happens".

Remember the previous chapter: a month earlier, the previous reporting justice had ordered everything sealed at the Supreme Court and then handed to the PGR. Now the police were asking for the normal path. Mendonça granted it, and the way he granted it is the interesting part. Instead of simply authorising, he wrote six pages on "compartmentalisation", the principle that investigative information circulates only among those who need it. He cites an academic article on the subject and then cites himself: a piece he published in 2019 in a Spanish journal of procedural law, transcribed in Spanish, on the "preservation of secrecy" and "functionality". The thesis, translated: access to data "is defined functionally, that is, it depends on the mission", and not "on competences or on organisations"; and improper use, "especially for political ends or to serve the interests of the media", is what is to be avoided.

From that come operating rules that apply to every proceeding of the operation under his charge. The Federal Police's internal-affairs office (Corregedoria) receives only what concerns the conduct of federal police officers. The Intelligence Directorate has a duty to pass on to the case's police chiefs, "and to them alone", whatever it learns, and receives back only what officers "with a direct and specific need to know" require, "which is not defined by their hierarchical position". The other areas of the police "are only to provide the means". Those involved have a duty of secrecy "including towards their hierarchical superiors and other public authorities". And a lock: "the opening of any new investigation or inquiry must first be expressly requested of this Reporting Justice, with reasons". He sets the secrecy at "level III" and orders the ruling copied into every Compliance Zero proceeding. The next day, a procedural order directs that this ruling be made public, and only this one.

The reader may find it excessive for a judge to write six pages of theory in order to authorise a forensic examination. But notice what the theory does: it takes the Intelligence Directorate and the top of the Federal Police out of the flow, and puts the case's police chiefs at the centre. After the January friction between the previous reporting justice and the Federal Police, it is a way of saying, without saying it, who is in charge of what.

## February and March: the safe-conducts

The congressional inquiry into organised crime (the CPMI) summoned Fabiano Zettel; he asked for a safe-conduct, and the reporting justice granted it, on 27 February, with the formula he would use dozens of times: the guarantee against self-incrimination "encompasses the option of appearing or not", since ADPFs 395 and 444 (actions for breach of a fundamental precept); appearance optional; if he goes, silence, a lawyer, no obligation to tell the truth. Five days later, Zettel would be placed in pre-trial detention in the other petition.

João Carlos Falbo Mansur, of REAG, was also summoned and also asked. But he made the request in two places at once: in this petition and in a habeas corpus that fell, by lot, to Justice Flávio Dino, who on 2 March granted a partial interim order guaranteeing silence, a lawyer and dispensation from the oath. On 9 March, Mendonça declared the requests here "moot", "to avoid the risk of conflicting decisions". It is a small reminder of how the Supreme Court works: the same right can be sought through two doors, and the one that opens first closes the other.

## 27 March: the lawyers want to read

A one-page procedural order lists twenty-one requests for access to the record, by document number, and sends them to the police and the PGR for comment "in the light of Binding Precedent 14". That precedent is the one that guarantees defence counsel access to the evidence "already documented". Twenty-one requests in two months is the measure of how many lawyers the second phase mobilised. The same order sends three requests for the return of property out for comment; those are chapter 5.

## 11 June: the drawer is only a drawer

The most important ruling of the period has six pages and solves a problem of architecture. The police pointed out that the petition concentrated "a high and complex volume of documents, to the detriment of the organic structure, the handling of the record and the proper overall understanding of its object", and that the São Paulo inquiry, whose jurisdiction had been declined to the STF, had never been docketed "in its own procedural class". They asked for the opening of a separate Inquiry, to which the main investigation would go, the petition keeping "exclusively" "the processing and monitoring of the precautionary measures".

Mendonça agreed, with two arguments that deserve to be recorded. The first is about the defence: the petition was undergoing "an objective widening of its scope through various requests, often made by investigated parties who do not even appear in the original police request"; separating the investigations protected "due process, full defence and the adversarial principle". The second is about secrecy, and it is the franker one: the petition had already been opened to the lawyers of the second-phase suspects, and "so as not to expose the secrecy of facts still under official investigation, as well as the privacy and intimacy of the parties", the investigation would continue "in a separate and autonomous proceeding". In translation: the lawyers read the drawer of precautionary measures; the investigation that continues goes into another drawer, a closed one.

Eight days later, on 19 June, more material arrived, this time "from proceedings originating in the 6th Federal Criminal Court", and the reporting justice ordered another inquiry docketed, because that material "bears no specific relation to the Second Phase". On 26 June, the Secretariat was ordered to "open a new proceeding for each of the cases" transmitted by the PGR by link. This is probably where some of the inquiries that today carry their own number in the archive come from, 5.026 and 5.035 among them; the record of this petition does not say which became which, and this text does not fill the gap.

## 6 July: the father, again

A two-page procedural order reveals a fact that is in no ruling of this petition. Henrique Moura Vorcaro submitted "clarifications on the dealings supposedly conducted by the petitioner with other alleged members of the criminal group called 'A Turma'", and asked for "the revocation of the pre-trial detention". His pre-trial detention, the order says, was ordered "within PET No. 15.978/DF" and confirmed by the Second Panel. Mendonça ordered the request removed from this record and attached there, "as a measure of rationalisation of the proceedings and to safeguard the secrecy of the petitioning party". Let it be recorded, then, what this record allows one to say and only that: in July 2026, Daniel Vorcaro's father was in pre-trial detention by an order made in another petition, linked to A Turma, and only the news of it reached this petition. What Petition 15.978 says is the subject of another series.

> Reading rule number ten: when a judge splits a proceeding in two, ask which half stayed open. The answer tells you what he wants the defence to read.
