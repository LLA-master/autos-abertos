Between 9 and 15 December 2025, Justice Dias Toffoli signed three decisions in constitutional complaint RCL 88.121, and the three share a feature that this site would rather point out than judge: each goes one step beyond what the precedent it cites allowed. This chapter is about that step, about the arguments that supported it and about what happened around it.

## The interim order

The decision of 9 December runs to 24 pages. The reporting justice records that, since 3 December, "any judicial measure must be assessed beforehand by this Court and no longer by the lower court", and that the defences of Vorcaro and Bull "narrate facts that show a concrete risk of the investigation being compromised": interviews scheduled, the 10th Panel of the TRF1 (the federal appeals court for the 1st region) set for 9 December to review the release, "sealed documents of the operation" that "have been improperly leaked to the press". He also records that Congressman João Carlos Paolilo Bacelar Filho had asked, on 4 December, for access to the file "by reason of the seizure of documents that mention him". The justice granted the access, "since he is referred to in the seized documents and responsible for the application to transfer jurisdiction". Note the verb: the party that applied for the transfer was Vorcaro's defence; the congressman only asked to read.

On the detention, the reporting justice deferred: since the TRF1 had already replaced pre-trial detention with alternative measures, "the assessment of whether to keep the measures other than detention is deferred, since full instruction of the case is considered indispensable, with the information from the authority complained of and the opinion of the Prosecutor General's Office". On jurisdiction, he decided.

The reasoning follows a line any student would recognise: privileged venue is not a privilege but a "bilateral guarantee, a guarantee against and in favour of the accused", in Victor Nunes Leal's phrase from 1962; the guarantee "also extends to the pre-trial stage"; and, in ADPF 424, of October 2025, the full bench had established that the seizure of documents that "have a bearing, even indirectly, on the exercise of the mandate" draws the jurisdiction to the Supreme Court "even if the person directly under investigation holds no parliamentary mandate". "Only the Supreme Federal Court has jurisdiction to determine which items of evidence may be used or set apart." The justice acknowledges the contrary case law, which requires "active and concrete participation by the holder of the prerogative", but concludes, "on preliminary examination", that "the seized items appear related to the exercise of the parliamentary mandate", which "may indicate a connection with an investigation under way at the Supreme Court or the commission of other ordinary crimes". And that "it is premature to accept the claim that the seized document would lack criminal relevance".

Here comes the step. Flávio Dino's precedent, from October, which the decision transcribes in full, said that the precautionary measure was "specific, proportionate and adequate", "not intending to suspend the progress of the investigation in relation to all the suspects, but only to set aside, temporarily, the continuation of the specific measures directed at the parliamentarian". Toffoli writes, in the next paragraph: "Notwithstanding the guidelines laid down in the precedent referred to, the concrete case imposes, unavoidably, the suspension of the investigative proceedings, in the lower courts, in relation to all the suspects, until sufficient clarification is reached as to the degree of involvement of the parliamentarian mentioned." The justification is the "apparent intertwining of the conducts" and the risk of "subsequent nullities". The justice insists that "the intention is not to paralyse the investigation, but to order the transfer of supervision of the subsequent acts to this Court", and cites cases in which entire investigations were annulled years later for having run before the wrong court.

The operative part, besides suspending and drawing up "all the subject matter, including related matter", kept "all the administrative, civil and criminal decisions issued so far in relation to all the suspects, including the restraints on goods and funds", and carried an order found in no precedent: "it is essential that the Central Bank of Brazil refrain from complying with any judicial decisions relating to the banking institutions involved in operation 'Compliance Zero' that do not come from this Supreme Federal Court". And it ordered the Central Bank to hand over "the documentation and the information relating to the administrative proceedings under way". What the Central Bank handed over is chapter 4.

## The congressional inquiry, the social network and the vault

On 4 December, the INSS congressional inquiry (CPMI) had approved the lifting of Vorcaro's telematic, bank and tax secrecy, and the secrecy of a company belonging to his brother-in-law. The defence asked the reporting justice of the complaint to annul all of it: a "fishing expedition", "what is the relevance of the Petitioner's WhatsApp messages to the improper deductions from pensioners?". On 11 December it added that "data and information from the investigations", "whose suspension and sealing were ordered" by the justice, "are being passed on, without judicial authorisation, to the senators who sit on the CPMI".

The decision of 12 December is, once again, a middle course nobody asked for. The reporting justice wrote, in capital letters, that "THIS IS NOT A CASE FOR GRANTING AN INTERIM ORDER ANNULLING THE DISCLOSURE ORDERS", and "I REITERATE THE GRANTING OF THE DISCLOSURE ORDERS DETERMINED BY THE CPMI". But, "as a measure of prudence", he ordered that the Central Bank, the tax authority and "the telematic companies" send the information "directly to the PRESIDENCY OF THE FEDERAL SENATE", "so that it remains there IN SAFEKEEPING until further deliberation by this Supreme Court". The disclosure orders stand; the data stay locked up.

Two side effects deserve a note. The first is the reply of X Brasil, the local arm of the former Twitter, to the inquiry's letter asking, going back to 2016, for "tweets, the accounts followed, favourite tweets; exact coordinates of the location of the tweets" and "links visited and how many times each was clicked". The company explained, over 26 pages, that the request "did not indicate which user", only a name and a taxpayer number (CPF), that "no CPF is collected for registration", and that, therefore, "it is not possible to identify users of the X platform". The lifting of a banker's telematic secrecy ran into the absence of a handle.

The second is more serious. The Senate's legal office (Advocacia do Senado), on behalf of the inquiry, filed a motion for clarification (*embargos*) in which it recounts that it learned of the decision "through the media", that it "received no communication with the content of the decision", and that, when the Senate executed the order, there was "the complete removal of the material relating to these disclosures from the JUBARTE vault system", including data "already lawfully received and stored by the CPMI until then". The committee "welcomes" the recognition that the disclosures are lawful, but points to the "logical incompatibility": if the disclosure order is valid, "there is no prima facie case (*fumaça de bom direito*) for granting other precautionary measures". And it recalls that congressional inquiries have a time limit, and that "keeping a precautionary measure that restricts the CPMI's access to the data may cause irreversible harm to the investigation, not through illegality but through the mere passage of time". There is no decision on that motion in the record.

## Those who knocked on the door

That same week, BRB asked to join the case. On 2 December it had hired a law firm and the consultancy Kroll for an "independent corporate investigation", and it wanted access to the "devices seized from BRB employees and former employees", including its then president. Two days later, it asked for the lifting of the freeze on twelve Master properties that had already been given to it as collateral, provisionally registered at the property registry on 7 November, eleven days before the freeze. Banco Pleno, controlled by Augusto Lima, asked for the annulment of the search carried out at its headquarters on Faria Lima: the warrant named Banco Master as its target, "towers A and B", and the team, on reaching the fifth floor of tower B, "recorded in the search record that a different financial institution operated there" and "went on with the search, collecting documents". None of those requests was decided in the complaint; chapter 5 tells where some of them resurfaced.

## The fifteenth of December

The third decision in the series is dated 15 December and runs to two pages. It says that "documents and information from various State bodies and also from the Judiciary" have arrived, that the "preliminary analysis" shows "the absolute need to carry out urgent measures, not only for the success of the investigations but also as a measure of protection for the National Financial System", and orders, "with an initial period of 30 days": the questioning of the suspects by a police chief appointed by the Director-General of the Federal Police; "the questioning of the officers of the Central Bank of Brazil on matters within their remit involving the activities of Banco Master and possible developments involving other financial institutions"; and the possibility of the police chief requesting, "on an individual basis", disclosure orders. The interviews "may take place by videoconference or in person in one of the hearing rooms located in this Supreme Court", "recorded and attended by the auxiliary judges of my chambers".

The reader who has followed the other series knows where that sentence led: the January interviews, the forensic reports, and, in March 2026, the Central Bank officials detained over informal consultancy. In December, the decision looked like routine. In hindsight, it was the moment when the complaint stopped being a case about a folder and became the chambers from which the investigation would be run.

> Reading rule number eighteen: watch the adverb "all". A precedent that says "only the parliamentarian" and a decision that says "all the suspects" are one word apart, and that word is the case.
