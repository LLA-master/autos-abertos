Some proceedings are born of a thousand-page report. Petition 15.504 was born of a conversation that nobody knows for certain how it went. What the record registers is that, on 7 January 2026, the Central Bank's president, Gabriel Galípolo, received a "verbal report presented by an interlocutor on condition of anonymity". The content: Belline Santana, head of the Bank Supervision Department, Desup, which inspects banks from the inside, and Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza, deputy head of the same department and former director of supervision, had allegedly received money from Daniel Vorcaro.

At 2:30 pm that day, the director of supervision, Ailton de Aquino Santos, was called to the president's office, where the bank's general counsel, Cristiano Cozer, was also present. Ailton would later tell the Federal Police that he answered the president that he "would take both men's statements that same day". And he did. At 5:30 pm, Belline was heard in the director's office. At 6:20 pm, Paulo Sérgio, by video.

What the two said that afternoon is in the "Detailed Account of Facts" (*Relato Circunstanciado de Fatos*) signed by Ailton and Cozer and attached to the crime report (*notícia-crime*). It is the shortest document in the petition and perhaps the most important, because it fixes the two versions before any lawyer.

## Two explanations

Belline said that, in 2023, a man called Leonardo Palhares, connected to an entity called Câmara-e.net, approached him saying he had been given his name because of his participation in an event of the IBGC, the corporate governance institute. Palhares proposed a project on financial inclusion for underprivileged young people, with a fee of R$ 2 million. Belline is said to have pointed out that he had no time; Palhares is said to have replied that he wanted "his name and his image". Belline stated that he received, in 2023, two half-yearly instalments of R$ 500,000, that he did in fact work on the project and took part in a podcast, that he asked more than once whether a financial institution was sponsoring it and was told no, that he grew "uncomfortable" and asked to stop in 2024, and that he came back in 2025, through a company called Inspiração, receiving R$ 100,000 a month "until the agreed amount was reached". Asked whether he knew that Banco Master might be behind the payments, he "categorically denied it".

Paulo Sérgio said that in 2019 someone turned up interested in buying, to subdivide into lots, the farm (*sítio*) he owned with his brother near Guaxupé, in southern Minas Gerais. He sold it in January 2020 for R$ 4.5 million, in five instalments, deposited in a joint account with his brother. A year later, at the time of the deed, he found out that the buyer was Daniel Vorcaro's brother-in-law. He stated that he did not know this at the time of the sale, that the price was the market price, that the buyer later gave up the subdivision, that his brother ended up leasing the same farm to keep growing coffee, and that all of it was in his income-tax returns.

Keep the two numbers in mind, R$ 2 million and R$ 4.5 million. They are going to change.

## Forty-eight hours

The next day, 8 January, the head of the bank's internal-affairs office (Corregedoria), Allyson Augusto Bastos, opened two asset inquiries, one for each official. His note acknowledges that the tip came without "documentary evidence", but argues that "the report of the receipt of substantial sums by officials holding senior commissioned posts" warranted investigation. On Belline's explanation, it observes that R$ 2 million "shows apparent disproportion to the services that would be rendered". On Paulo Sérgio's, that selling a farm to the brother-in-law of "a person known to be involved in serious facts under investigation" was enough on its own to investigate.

On the same 8 January, Ailton signed the precautionary removal of the two from their posts for sixty days, without loss of salary, invoking article 45 of the administrative procedure law, which allows a precautionary step without hearing the person concerned "in case of imminent risk". The justification: "to avoid any risk to the effectiveness of the supervision of the financial system".

On the 9th, the bank's legal department produced Opinion 31/2026, nine pages long, to answer a question that looks bureaucratic and is not: report to whom? The law requires the Central Bank to notify the prosecution service when it comes across a crime in the exercise of its functions. The opinion, citing internal precedents, concluded that, when the elements are "incipient", the proper addressee is the Federal Police, "primarily suited to exercising the investigative powers typical of still early stages". The crime contemplated, "at least on a preliminary assessment": passive bribery.

Galípolo signed the letter to the Federal Police's director general that same 9 January. At 6:50 pm, the bank's executive secretary sent everything by e-mail to the duty desk of the Director General's office, with the subject "[CONFIDENTIAL] Notification letter" (*[SIGILOSO] Ofício comunicação*) and high priority. On Monday the 12th, the chief of staff forwarded the case to the Coordination of Inquiries in the Higher Courts, "considering a possible connection with the object of inquiry 2025.0087917", the internal number of the investigation the public knows as Operation Compliance Zero.

## The phone

The police took five weeks to respond, and the response came from somewhere else. On 18 February, police chief Victor Arruda de Oliveira ordered the Central Bank's report to be registered, forwarded to the head of the financial division, and a "draft request to be submitted to the Reporting Justice of INQ 5026, with a view to severing this specific situation" to be prepared. On the same 18 February, five police officers signed Police Intelligence Report 144738439.2026: 131 pages on an iPhone 17 Pro seized from Vorcaro on the day of his arrest, in November 2025. The document's hypothesis, which is the subject of the next chapter, is that the banker exercised "improper influence" over the two officials and granted them "improper advantages", with "financial operators" handling the money's route.

On 23 February, the request reached the Supreme Court. It is five pages long. It asks for the opening of a sealed petition, separate from Inquiry 5.026, "intended exclusively for the investigation of the facts related to the possible commission of bribery and related crimes involving senior officials of the Central Bank". The argument is one of organisation: the parent inquiry has "a broad thematic scope", and the officials' cell has "its own dynamics, a delimited chronology and a need for autonomous measures", among them disclosure orders, interviews and "possible cooperation with the Central Bank itself and the CGU". The crimes listed, "in theory": passive and active bribery, money laundering, criminal organisation, breach of official secrecy and, "depending on how the evidence develops", crimes against the financial system. The petition was docketed at 6:13 pm and assigned, by prior connection (*prevenção*), to Justice André Mendonça at 7:51 pm.

## The Supreme Court says yes, slowly

The reporting justice took five weeks for the first order. On 27 March, he sent the file to the Prosecutor General's Office for comment and, "outright", denied access to the record to two sets of lawyers who had asked to be admitted to the docket: those of Marilson Roseno da Silva and those of Leonardo Palhares, "since their clients are not under investigation in this specific case". Palhares, the man Belline said had approached him, was not under investigation at the Supreme Court in March. He would be, at the police, from May.

The PGR answered within three days. The Prosecutor General, Paulo Gonet, wrote that the elements "denote the existence of sufficient elements for the opening of a specific inquiry", that there are "consistent indications" of "informal and unofficial" contacts between Vorcaro and the two officials, and that the whole, "comprising the granting of improper financial and property advantages, the officials' acting on behalf of private interests, by reviewing drafts and documents that would be officially sent to the Central Bank, in a kind of consultancy", made "a deeper investigation imperative". He asked for an inquiry to be opened "against Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza and Belline Santana", with the seal maintained.

Mendonça ruled on 30 April, a month later. The order has two pages and three movements: it summarises the request, records "the acquiescence of the Prosecutor General's Office" and authorises, on the basis of article 21, XV, of the Court's internal rules, "the opening of an investigative procedure, linked to INQ 5026". One notes that the justice's order describes the hypothesis as crimes committed "by DANIEL BUENO VORCARO jointly with the officials", whereas the PGR's opinion asked for an inquiry against the two officials. It is a difference of framing, not of decision; the police used the wider one.

On 14 May, police chief Albert Paulo Sérvio de Moura opened police inquiry 2026.0053200. The opening order (*portaria*) sums up the hypothesis in one paragraph worth reading in full, because it is the accusation in its rawest form: the two officials "allegedly received improper economic advantages" from Vorcaro, from Palhares, from Fabiano Zettel and from Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva "in exchange for directing the supervision and inspection actions of the federal authority towards the interests of Banco Master"; allegedly performed official acts "aimed at making possible the sale of loan portfolios known to be false by Banco Master to Banco Regional de Brasília"; allegedly reviewed Master documents addressed to the Central Bank itself and to the FGC; allegedly passed on confidential information from the Central Bank and the CVM; and allegedly brokered the purchase of other banks. To hide the origin of the money, says the opening order, the payers "simulated, in theory, service contracts and property sale contracts" and used "layers of intermediary legal entities": Varajo, Super, Moriah, Pipe.

The opening order summons Ailton as a witness and, as suspects, Belline, Paulo Sérgio, Palhares and Ana Cláudia; Zettel and Vorcaro would be heard by video, from the units where they were held.

## What the petition is

In September 2026, Petition 15.504 has 93 files and 6,374 pages. Almost all of it is the police inquiry and its twelve annexed volumes, sent to the Supreme Court in July and in September. From the court itself, there is one ruling and four orders, all of one or two pages: sending the file to the PGR, authorising the inquiry, extending the deadline by sixty days on 13 July and again on 4 September, and sending the PGR, for comment, a request from Paulo Sérgio that his statement no longer be sealed. The rest are admissions of counsel and the PGR's acknowledgements.

It is, therefore, a proceeding in which the judge hardly speaks and the police speak all the time. The series follows that proportion. The next chapter is about the phone; the two after it, about the money and about the inquiry; the last two, about what each of those heard said, because in this proceeding, unlike the earlier ones, the suspects spoke, and spoke a great deal.

> Reading rule number twenty-eight: a detailed account is not a statement. It was written by the one who listened, not by the one who spoke. When the same man gives his version to the police chief, six months later, the numbers will change. That does not prove a lie; it proves that the memory of an office conversation is not a set of minutes.
