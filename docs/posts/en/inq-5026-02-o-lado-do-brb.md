Every fraud needs two ends, and the public narrative of the Banco Master case has a habit of looking at only one. Inquiry 5.026, however, was opened, in the judge's own words, "to investigate supposed crimes against the National Financial System committed in the context of the acquisition of Banco Master by Banco de Brasília". BRB is not scenery: it is a party. This chapter is about what the November 2025 ruling says of the buyer, and about the question it leaves open: why a state-owned bank, with the Central Bank already asking, kept on paying.

As always, what follows is what appears in requests and precautionary rulings, with the defence yet to be heard on the merits. The BRB executives named were not detained; they were removed from office, and the removal lasted as long as chapter 3 tells.

## Thirty percent

The judge's starting point is a proportion: BRB acquired "loan portfolios that amounted to 30% of all its assets", which, he writes, "constitutes a relevant indication that the state-owned institution sought to prop up Banco Master in its liquidity crisis". The investigation wants to know "the reasons why BRB disregarded serious irregularities in the operations, including situations in which the deposit receipt did not match the value of the Bank Credit Note, a picture that persisted even after three months of supervision".

The record uses two numbers. The first, from the Central Bank: R$ 12.2 billion transferred "from January to May 2025", R$ 6.7 billion for the portfolios and R$ 5.5 billion as premium. The second, from the Federal Police, broader: "between July 2024 and 3 October 2025 alone, the equivalent of 16,717,138,715.05 billion reais was transferred to the Master group by BRB". The odd wording, "16 billion billion", is the original's; the number is R$ 16.7 billion. That is the "level" up to which the police asked for a daily freeze on accounts, and it is where the figure of "R$ 17 billion" comes from, the one the congressional inquiry committee (CPMI) would later use to summon people.

## What the Central Bank itself saw at BRB

The harshest passage against BRB is not the police's; it is from the Central Bank's technical staff, in a document on "prudential measures" that the ruling transcribes. The four items are worth reading. First: "in the first half of 2025, after an assessment carried out after the acquisition, BRB found that part of the portfolio had characteristics, including its formalisation, incompatible with the criteria for keeping these assets". It bought, then it looked. Second: "impacts on BRB's liquidity are observed from the third quarter of 2024 onwards"; in the returns, "there is replacement by new portfolios or investment-fund quotas, without a return of the financial flow", and there were "moments when the level fell below the internally established liquidity buffer". Third: "there was a breach of the Basel ratio in the months of January and February 2025", and, "to avoid the breach, BRB made accounting entries without documentary support, relying on a contract for the sale of its equity stake in BRB Financeira, executed only in March 2025", an issue "still not remedied". Fourth: on 30 June 2025 "there was an inconsistency in the information on the overdue portfolio (R$ 10.0 billion)".

The judge sums up: "the transfers have been carried out since 2024, even in the face of the reservations expressed by the Central Bank". And frames it: "strong indications of the crime of fraudulent management by BRB's managers in collusion with the Directors of Banco Master". Let it be noted that BRB had signed with the Central Bank, on 10 February 2025, a commitment agreement (*Termo de Compromisso*), "after being caught supplying documents, data and information at odds with the rules". The ruling uses this as proof that "BRB's conduct as regards failures in credit governance is repeated".

## The limit nobody could cross

The most elegant explanation in the case, for those not from the market, is in the section on the "single-client exposure limit", the LEC. A bank cannot lend to a single client more than a fraction of its capital. Master, with tier 1 capital of R$ 4.5 billion in 2024, had a limit of "approximately 1.1 billion", "a fact that prevented it from, for instance, standing as guarantor of the operations carried out with BRB, which is why the portfolios were formally assigned without recourse". BRB, with R$ 3.012 billion of tier 1 capital in December 2024, had a limit of "about R$ 753 million", which "prevented it from making an interbank deposit or a loan to Banco Master equivalent to 12.2 billion".

Then comes the police's calculation: "to support exposures of the magnitude of the amounts transferred by BRB, 12.2 billion, the related counterparties would need to have at least R$ 48.8 billion in Tier 1 Capital, an amount clearly incompatible with the reality of mid-sized financial institutions". And the consequence: when BRB unwound the deal with Tirreno and "replaced the portfolios with other Banco Master assets, in order to amortise the 12.2 billion", that "meant a breach of the exposure limit", because the exposure was now to Master itself, whose limit "ran at around 1.125 billion". In plain language: buying a third party's portfolio without recourse was the way to lend to Master without it looking like a loan. The police hypothesis, which the judge calls "viable and plausible", is on page 85 of the police request: "BRB's initial wish was always to lend money to Banco Master, but it could not do so directly because of exposure limits".

## Three months and a clause

The chronology of BRB's reply to the Central Bank is what weighs most heavily against its executives in the ruling. Supervision began on 17 March 2025. BRB "stated its position on the origin of the loans on 18 June 2025, 3 (three) months after the request for clarification", by then already pointing to Tirreno, and "concludes the submission by citing the replacement of the loans and the unwinding of the deal". But, the judge writes, "only after the R$ 12.2 billion had been transferred did BRB conclude that the deal should be unwound", and "even after becoming aware that the operations were being monitored by the Central Bank, BRB continued to make transfers running into billions": "between April and May 2025, portfolios of CCBs totalling 4.05 billion were assigned, with the corresponding transfer of premium".

The partnership contract had a termination clause: if Tirreno failed to carry out an audit, the indemnity "was to be paid immediately on the first business day thereafter". What was agreed instead was "a return in monthly tranches entirely foreign to the contractual provisions". And, on the eve of the first instalment, Master put on the record "the statement of Tirreno's account, with the full balance": the R$ 6.7 billion was there, in an escrow account, inside Master. The judge notes that "the lack of an immediate demand by BRB for the deposited amounts is strange". The replacement of the portfolios, in the police's phrase that the ruling adopts, "took place out of pure camaraderie" (*pura camaradagem*).

There is also the complaint by the association of BRB's employees to the CVM, which became a sanctioning proceeding, alleging "the absence of an adequate informational basis for the operation", "fragility in governance" and "instability of leadership". The employees of the state-owned bank complained about the deal before the police did.

## The two executives

The November ruling treats BRB's executives differently from Master's, and the difference is instructive. For Paulo Henrique Bezerra Rodrigues Costa, the president, and Dario Oswaldo Garcia Junior, the finance director, the judge recognises "indications of authorship" of fraudulent management and "the decision to rescue Banco Master", but writes that "there are no concrete elements of initial participation in the structure of the criminal organisation"; their adherence "came later, which reveals serious but sporadic conduct". They "are not partners and controllers of various funds", "do not control shelf companies", were not "capable of procuring false loan contracts". Their dangerousness "is considerably lower". Result: precautionary removal from office for 60 days, passport withheld, monthly reporting. No detention.

The judge also transcribes the explanation BRB's president gave the MPF, that the bank "sought to expand its business and increase its profitability", in a "strategy of asset diversification", and that the portfolios "would have been acquired from third parties, and not generated in retail". And he adopts the MPF's rejoinder: had they been bought from third parties, "they would be recorded in the stock"; and payroll lending to civil servants is "a restricted market", with known payroll agents, "which makes that intermediation unnecessary". The defence of BRB's executives, in the record of this series, does not yet have the final word; the 60-day removal, the Central Bank never converted into an intervention, as chapter 3 tells.

## Why this chapter exists

Because the Banco Master case, as told by the press, is the case of a banker. As told by the record of Inquiry 5.026, it is the case of two banks, one of them state-owned, and of a regulator that asked in March and only got an answer in June. The money the police say was diverted is the money of a state bank of the Federal District, whose majority shareholder is the local government. In November the judge ordered the Central Bank to diagnose "the real state of this financial institution". The audit that replaced that order, and what came of it, lies outside this record. The question remains, and it is the inquiry's own: who, at BRB, decided to pay, and knowing what.

> Reading rule number thirteen: follow the buyer. The seller of a bad portfolio needs someone who will buy without looking, and it is in that someone that an investigation usually finds the decision that matters.
