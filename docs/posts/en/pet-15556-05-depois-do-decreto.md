A detention order does not close a petition; it opens it. After 3 March 2026, Petition 15.556 received fifteen more rulings and sixteen procedural orders from the reporting justice, each of them small, each of them solving a problem the big ruling had created. Read in sequence, they tell the life of a case: where the detainee sleeps, who may visit him, whether he may have a television, whether the congressional inquiry may summon him, whether a suspect's family receives his pension, what is done when a suspect dies. This chapter is that sequence, in order, with dates. It is the least dramatic of the series and the most revealing of how the system works once the cameras have gone.

## 4 March: where the detainees are held

The first ruling after the order is about prison logistics. The Federal Police reported that it had executed the warrants and that the detainees were in the Detainee Transit Units of its regional headquarters, structures "intended for transitory custody of very short duration", without regular medical care, visits or psychosocial support, and asked for authorisation to take them to the state prison system. The justice granted it the same day, observing that the original ruling did not require continuous custody at the PF. At least two suspects had different destinations soon afterwards.

On the same 4 March, the PF reported an arrest in flagrante: on detaining Luiz Phillipi Machado de Moraes Mourão, a firearm of his was allegedly found "without his presenting any valid document for carrying or owning it". The justice ordered the same custody-hearing rules to be applied to the new fact and asked for the file back "immediately" to decide whether to convert the in flagrante arrest into pre-trial detention. He never got to decide. Two days later, Mourão died.

## 5 March: the federal penitentiary

The PF asked for Daniel Vorcaro to be transferred from a state prison in São Paulo to the Federal Penitentiary in Brasília. The legal basis is Law 11.671, which reserves the maximum-security federal system for detainees for whom the measure "is justified in the interest of public safety or of the detainee himself". The police argument, transcribed in the ruling, is twofold and worth reading carefully because it says how the PF sees the suspect: he "holds a significant capacity for articulation and influence over various actors in different spheres of public power and the private sector", able to "mobilise networks of influence" to interfere in the investigation; and, "for the same reasons", federal custody would serve to "better guarantee the physical integrity of the detainee himself". The Federal Penitentiary, it adds, is close to the bodies conducting the investigation. The justice accepted in one page: the context "fits" the law. Immediate transfer, with escort and notice to SENAPPEN.

## 6 March: the death

Mourão's defence informed the court, with a certificate, that he had died on 6 March 2026. The circumstances are not in the ruling that deals with the matter, and this text does not invent them. What is there is the legal outcome, decided on 29 May, with a favourable opinion from the PGR: the State's power to punish extinguished under article 107, I, of the Penal Code, because "the death of the agent constitutes an objective cause for the exclusion of the State's jus puniendi". Partial closure of the investigations as regards him.

But the justice made three reservations that change the meaning of the closure. First: the asset freezes remain, because "the obligation to repair any damage, not being a penalty but a measure of asset recovery, passes to the heirs"; the heirs are "urged to enter the proceedings". Second: the extinction "is no obstacle" to future orders lifting the deceased's secrecy, "if necessary to clarify the offences involving the criminal organisation in which, in theory, he took part". Third, implicit in the previous one: Mourão's messages remain in the record as evidence against the others. The dead man leaves the case as a defendant and stays as an involuntary witness.

## 9 March: the lawyers and the camera

Vorcaro asked the justice to have the Federal Penitentiary allow daily visits by his lawyers, without appointment, "without any kind of monitoring or audio and/or video recording", with copies of the file allowed in and the right to take notes. According to the petition, the prison's management had said the meeting would be recorded and that counsel would not enter the interview room "even with paper and pen".

The ruling is a small exercise in statutory reading. The same Law 11.671 requires audio and video monitoring of interview rooms and common areas in federal prisons, but expressly excludes "legal consultations, unless expressly authorised otherwise by a court". The justice saw no reason for the exception and granted everything, with the proviso that the administration may adopt "the necessary precautionary measures" for security. The PGR appealed that ruling, and later withdrew, "the matter having become moot". In July, Fabiano Zettel asked for something similar for himself, consultations in a private room, plus a television in his cell. The justice refused both: the existing interview room is adequate, said the PGR, and the ruling agrees; and a television in a cell is "a matter inherent to the administrative functioning of the penitentiary", with no "autonomous subjective right". The same already applied to Vorcaro, the ruling notes. Equality among prisoners, in Brazil, sometimes shows itself in the denial of a television set.

## March and April: the congressional inquiry

The Joint Congressional Inquiry Committee on Organised Crime (CPMI) summoned, in succession, four people connected to this record: Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva, Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza, Belline Santana and, in April, Ibaneis Rocha Barros Júnior, the latter because of, according to the summons transcribed in the ruling, the "business relations of the law firm he founded" with entities under investigation and the "institutional role played by the Governor in the strategic decisions of Banco de Brasília (BRB)", whose transactions with Master, the same text says, "constitute the central object of the investigations". Ibaneis is not a suspect in this petition; the ruling recognises his "status as a person under investigation" only "on the strength of the justification presented by the committee", that is, on what the CPMI said about him when it summoned him.

The four rulings are almost identical, and the repetition is the point. All begin by acknowledging "the superlative importance" of the CPMI and all conclude the same thing: whoever is treated as a suspect has the right not to incriminate himself, and that right, since ADPFs 395 and 444 of 2018, includes the option of not appearing. Attendance becomes optional; if the person goes, he has the right to silence, to a lawyer, to refuse to swear to tell the truth and to be free from constraint. For those wearing ankle monitors, the PF handles transport with escort and the Senate's Legislative Police handles custody inside Congress. A human detail slips out: the CPMI reports, "on the basis of press reports", that Paulo Sérgio was complying with the measures "at a residence outside this Federal Capital, in the municipality of Guaxupé/MG". The deputy head of Desup, with an ankle monitor, in the interior of Minas Gerais.

## May and June: the father and the clerk

On 29 May, the justice ruled on the request of Henrique Moura Vorcaro, Daniel's father, holder of the account at REAG where the second phase of the operation froze R$ 2.245 billion. His defence raised "alleged reputational harm" from the press coverage, asked for access to the file and explained that the funds have no "immediate liquidity", because they are said to be paid into a private-equity fund "tied to carbon-credit assets pending realisation", with no "causal or financial link whatsoever with contributions from Banco Master". The justice, following the PGR, refused, without saying he is wrong: he said the discussion "belongs to the merits themselves", that "certainty as to the lawful or unlawful origin of the funds is not required" at this stage, and kept the freeze "without prejudice to later analysis". It is the most important ruling in the chapter for anyone following the money: two billion remain frozen, and the explanation that they are carbon credits remains unjudged.

On 3 June came Marilson Roseno da Silva, the crew's retired police officer, in detention, whose accounts were frozen by order of another petition, 15.563. He asked for the release of his pension account, or for the benefit to be paid to his wife, to support his wife and minor daughter. The PGR was willing to release one minimum wage a month. The justice went further on one point and less far on another: he refused to unfreeze the accounts, because they hold sums "apparently related to the commission of offences", but allowed the pension of a "Federal Police Clerk", which "has, from what has been ascertained so far, no relation whatsoever to the offences", to be paid into his wife's account. Maintenance income, says the ruling, enjoys "reinforced legal protection". The same case that calls the crew a militia pays the clerk's pension. It is not a contradiction; it is the rule of law working at two speeds.

## 24 July: the CVM and the CGU

The last two rulings in the record, from the same day, show other bodies coming in through the door the March ruling opened. The securities commission (CVM) asked to question Vorcaro, in detention, in three administrative investigations: one about Ambipar Participações e Empreendimentos and its controllers, in the spot and derivatives markets in 2024 and 2025; two about commercial notes and real-estate receivables certificates bought by funds with acronyms for names, CITY-02 FIDC, CITY FIDC, MÁXIMA FIM Crédito Privado 2, MN I FIDC, ST 1001 FIDC. The justice authorised the questioning, in person at the penitentiary or by video, in August or September, with a lawyer present and the right to "total or partial" silence. The PGR asked only that it be made clear that the statement is voluntary, because whatever is said to the CVM "may be forwarded to the prosecution service".

The federal comptroller (CGU) asked for the evidence to be shared for an asset inquiry and possible liability proceedings against the two Central Bank officials and the five companies. Granted, with the proviso of the General Data Protection Law: use "exclusively for the purposes of instructing the administrative proceedings indicated".

## 10 and 11 September: the light

At the request of the STF's presidency, the reporting justice lifted the seal on 10 September; on the 11th the Court published the package this site is made of. Petition 15.556, when it became public, had 492 filings and 2,564 pages. This and the four previous chapters dealt with 132 of them, the single-justice rulings. The rest is petitions, receipts, powers of attorney, annexes, and it is where the defence speaks. This site has not read it for you. But it says where it is.

> Reading rule number six: what happens after the big ruling is what tells whether it was an act or a process. Here it was a process: every request got an answer, every answer had a reason, and not all of them went the accuser's way.
