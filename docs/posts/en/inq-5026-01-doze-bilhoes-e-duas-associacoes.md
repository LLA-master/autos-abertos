Before it became an operation, the Banco Master case was a letter. On 17 March 2025 the Central Bank's bank-supervision department, Desup, sent Master Letter 7062/2025 (*Ofício 7062/2025*) asking for information about loan portfolios traded with BRB, the state-owned bank of the Federal District. The reason, according to the court ruling that months later would sum everything up, was "an atypical flow of funds, totalling R$ 12.2 billion, in the first months of 2025, from BRB to Banco Master, exceeding the state-owned bank's exposure limit". Master's reply to that letter, on 25 March, is the document that gives rise to Inquiry 5.026. This series is about it: the inquiry that was born in Brasília, put the bank's directors in custody in November 2025, went up to the Supreme Court in December and, when the STF opened the record to the public, was still open.

The usual warning, here with an addition: everything that follows is what the Federal Police, the prosecution service, the Central Bank and a first-instance judge wrote in requests and precautionary rulings. The defences contested almost all of it, some with documents, and chapter 3 gives them the space the record gives them. Nobody had been indicted in this inquiry as of the date it became public.

## The reply

Master answered the Central Bank saying that the loans assigned to BRB originated with two associations of civil servants of the State of Bahia, ASTEBA and ASSEBA. The Central Bank went to check. It found, according to the "brief account of the events" (*relato sucinto das ocorrências*) it sent to the prosecution service, that "from 13 January 2025 onwards, the loan assignments began to involve CPFs from various parts of the country", not only Bahia, and that "no financial movements by the said Associations compatible with the loan assignments to Master were found". The MPF wrote to the government of Bahia: the deductions the associations make from civil servants' pay slips "relate to low-value membership fees and association services", and "could not have originated the R$ 6.7 billion in payroll-loan portfolios acquired by BRB".

Questioned again, Master changed its answer. The origin of the loans was now attributed to a company called Tirreno Consultoria Promotoria de Crédito e Participações S.A. That switch of versions, from two Bahian associations to a consultancy, is the fact the judge would later call "the creation of untruthful information and obstruction of the supervisory work of BACEN". It is, in short, the central accusation of the inquiry: not that the bank had problems, but that it allegedly lied to its supervisor about where the loans it was selling to the state-owned bank came from.

## The hypothesis

The Federal Police's investigative hypothesis, transcribed in the November ruling, is a story about liquidity. Master, the police say, issued "approximately R$ 50 billion in CDBs and DIs", and "12 billion would possibly be uncovered", because the bank's portfolio "would be backed mostly by low-liquidity assets". Its historical retail loan origination "ran at around R$ 200-300 million a month", and its stock of portfolios, "about R$ 2.0 billion", did not cover the "more than 30 billion needed to settle the maturing CDBs". To raise money far above what it produced, the group allegedly decided "to associate itself, unlawfully, with a direct-credit company, with the aim of inflating its assets artificially, through the acquisition of non-existent loan portfolios and their resale to BRB".

The sentence is the police's and it is a strong one: "non-existent loan portfolios". The reader should read it as a hypothesis, because that is what the text itself calls it. What supports the hypothesis comes next.

## The shelf company

Tirreno, according to the ruling, "was called SX 016 Empreendimentos e Participações S.A., created on 4 November 2024". On 2 December 2024 its articles of association were allegedly amended: new name, new economic activity, capital increased "from R$ 100.00 to R$ 30,000,000.00", and the original director replaced by André Felipe de Oliveira Seixas Maia, "a former Master employee". The person who appears as responsible for paying in the thirty million is Henrique Souza e Silva Peretto, "a partner and significant shareholder of Cartos", the direct-credit company. Three days after the change, on 5 December 2024, Tirreno signed with Master a "Partnership Agreement and Other Covenants" (*Contrato de parceria e outras avenças*) for the acquisition of the portfolios that would be resold to BRB.

Except that, according to the commercial registry (*Junta Comercial*), the change of name was only registered on 15 April 2025, "after the start of the supposed assignments and after the first version of the assignment given to BACEN". On the date the contracts were signed, therefore, "officially the company still belonged" to the previous director "and was called SX 016". The previous director, according to the police, "opened several other CNPJs with the same characteristics", at the same address, and is linked to an advisory firm that "displays on its website" the offer of "shelf companies". A shelf company is a company created empty to be sold ready-made to whoever is in a hurry. If Tirreno was that, it is no crime; the inquiry's question is what was done with it.

The MPF adds the dating of the papers: the initial partnership contract is not "even" notarised, and the assignment instruments, "dated between 3 January 2025 and 27 March 2025, were notarised only on 14 May 2025, at the same notary's office", as were the documents delivered to the Central Bank. The MPF calls this "serial forgery of documents, to meet the requests of the authority's technical staff". The judge, "possible document fraud". The difference between the two expressions is the difference between accusing and deciding a precautionary measure.

## What the Central Bank tested

The most technical part, and the most persuasive for the reader, is what the Central Bank did to check whether the loans existed. It selected "30 supposed borrower clients (CPFs) of the operations assigned in January 2025", at random, and looked at "all financial movements in favour of those clients" by TED and Pix since 2020. For loans taken by those same clients with other institutions, it managed to match "almost all" to the respective disbursements. For the operations sold by Master to BRB, "it was not possible to establish any correspondence between the operations and the respective financial flows for any of the 30 clients in the sample".

Then BRB handed over a sample of 100 contracts, with proof of payroll registration and deposit. The Central Bank listed the problems: on the notes, "the credit details (bank, branch and account) are not filled in"; the date of the deposit "is later than the date of issue of the CCB (on average 180 days)"; the value of the note "is, in general, significantly lower than the value of the deposit"; and all the operations "would have been originated by Cartos". Consulted, Cartos replied that those receipts referred to operations "assigned by Cartos to three receivables funds named Noto, Sueste and VCK, and not to Tirreno or to Banco Master". In other words: the document that was supposed to prove the loan belonged to Tirreno proved, according to the originator itself, that it belonged to someone else.

There is a statistical detail worth a paragraph of argument. The examination of the assignments of 13, 16, 20 and 30 January 2025 "revealed a substantial quantity of operations in distinct sets of only 12 values (182 thousand loan operations with 151 thousand holders)". One hundred and eighty-two thousand loans, and only twelve different values. Real payroll lending looks like people: each one asks for an amount. Manufactured lending looks like a spreadsheet.

And Tirreno itself: "Tirreno's only relationship within the National Financial System is with Master"; "no financial movements by Tirreno were identified in a query of the payment systems (TEDs, PIX, bank slips or foreign exchange)". An originator of R$ 6.7 billion in loans that never made a single Pix transfer. The Central Bank's conclusion, numbered as item 31: the operations "show indications of insubstantiality, signalling the existence of possible accounting and financial engineering to enable Master to raise funds from BRB".

## The accounting of the premium

One last point from the Central Bank, for those who like accounting. BRB paid Master R$ 12.2 billion, "R$ 6.7 billion for the portfolios and R$ 5.5 billion as premium", that is, goodwill. Master "did not book the R$ 5.5 billion premium as income, nor as deferred income", which would have been the two possible ways; it used "a third option, not provided for in the accounting rules, which was the adjustment (reduction) of an asset", a line called "Sundry Debtors" (*Devedores Diversos*), "whose amounts bear no relation whatsoever to the operations carried out". Five and a half billion of gain that does not show up as gain. It is the kind of entry an auditor marks in red and an investigator marks in another colour.

## What this is not yet

All of the above was presented to a federal judge in Brasília in October and November 2025, in an inquiry opened at the MPF's request and assigned to the 10th Federal Criminal Court on 18 August 2025, after the CVM had filed a criminal complaint on 24 June and the Central Bank had sent its report to the prosecution service. The judge found all this sufficient to detain five people. An appeals court, ten days later, found it was not. And, when the record went up to the Supreme Court, the defences presented documents that attack central premises of that narrative, including who was at the bank on each date. This series is not going to pretend the story ends in chapter 1. It merely begins here, with a letter, a reply and a second reply.

> Reading rule number twelve: when a suspect changes the version given to the regulator, the case stops being about accounting and becomes about trust. That is why the second reply weighs more than the first.
