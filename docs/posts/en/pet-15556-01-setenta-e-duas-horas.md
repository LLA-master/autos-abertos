A Supreme Court proceeding called a "Petition" is not a letter. It is a drawer. In it are kept requests that need a judge before they become something else: an inquiry, an indictment, nothing. Petition 15.556 is the drawer in which the Federal Police, on 27 February 2026, deposited 164 pages asking Justice André Mendonça for four detentions, four ankle monitors, two suspensions from public office and the closure of five companies. Five days later, on 3 March, the answer came out: 48 pages, 125 numbered items, one signature. That ruling is what this series is about. It is the document that gives the public case its name and it is, at the same time, the most read and the least read in the record: everyone cites it, almost nobody opens it.

Let us open it, then. With a warning that the ruling itself gives and which is worth repeating on the first page: nothing here is a judgment. It is a precautionary measure, taken, in the justice's words, on a "summary assessment" (*cognição sumária*), that is, with what was to hand that week. He cites Professor Gustavo Badaró to recall that "the question of certainty is foreign to precautionary proceedings". Whoever reads a pre-trial detention as a conviction is reading the wrong document.

## The request

The Federal Police request arrived grounded on articles 311 and 312 of the Code of Criminal Procedure and with a list of seven headings, which the ruling reproduces in its second paragraph: crimes against the financial system, active and passive bribery, criminal organisation, money laundering, breach of official secrecy, procedural fraud and obstruction of justice. Seven headings for a story the PF summarises thus, and the ruling endorses "on a summary assessment": Banco Master, controlled by Daniel Bueno Vorcaro, is alleged to have set up a scheme of aggressive fundraising through CDBs with yields far above the market, and to have poured the money into high-risk operations, illiquid assets and funds linked to its own group.

So far, a bank case. What changes the literary genre is the next page. According to the police authority, the scheme had four "cells": a financial one, in charge of the frauds; one of "institutional corruption", aimed at co-opting Central Bank officials; one of asset concealment and laundering, with front companies; and one of "intimidation and obstruction of justice", responsible, the text says, for the illegal monitoring of adversaries, journalists and public officials. This fourth cell had the name of a carnival block: "A Turma" (the crew). The ruling describes the crew as a "structure of private surveillance and coercion". Keep the phrase. It comes back in chapter 2.

## The seventy-two hours

Here begins the part that only those who read the record know. On 27 February the justice sent the file to the federal prosecution service for comment within 72 hours. The deadline expired on 2 March at 8:27 pm, *in albis*, which is how the law says "with nobody saying anything". On the 3rd, with the deadline already past, the Prosecutor General's Office filed a petition that the ruling transcribes. The PGR's argument deserves careful reading, because it is the only institutional counterpoint that exists within the document.

The PGR said that a deadline "counted in hours" was "impossible to meet for the holder of the criminal action", that the request involved "ten individuals and five legal entities" in "facts of high complexity", that these were "drastic measures, of the most intense interference with the most elementary fundamental rights", and that no "imminent, immediate danger" could be seen that would justify an analysis "so quick and necessarily cursory". It asked, therefore, that everything wait. And it closed with the sentence that weighs most: before that analysis, the PGR "cannot be in favour of the precautionary requests, and cannot endorse them".

The justice refused the extension in item 8, on the third page, with a sentence from someone who knows he will explain later: I refer the reasons "to the decisional item devoted to the analysis of the merits". The hasty reader thinks that is all. It is not. The reason appears 27 pages further on, in item 91.1, and it is one of the most personal passages of the ruling. The verb is "one regrets" (*lamenta-se*), repeated. One regrets that the PGR did not see the imminent danger, because, says the reporting justice, the evidence and the urgency "are abundantly revealed" in the request; because there was "the concrete possibility of preventing possible unlawful conduct against the physical and moral integrity of ordinary citizens, of a journalist and even of public officials"; and because there were "indications of improper access to the confidential systems of the Federal Police, of the federal prosecution service itself and even of international bodies such as Interpol". And he finishes in the Latin of urgency: "tempus fugite". Delay, he writes, "proves extremely dangerous for society".

Let the scene be recorded, because it is rare: the body that prosecutes asks for calm; the judge who decides says that calm is a risk. It is not the usual script. That is why it is here.

## Where the haste came from

The ruling is not the first act of the case, and it reconstructs the before itself, transcribing the Federal Police. Vorcaro had already been detained and released: the earlier detention was revoked by the TRF1 (the federal appeals court for the 1st region) on 28 November 2025, with an express warning that any act against public order, the investigation or the criminal law could reinstate the order. According to the PF, what was discovered afterwards is that the acts of obstruction "were not only under way, but are in full development".

The PF cites the second phase of Operation Compliance Zero, launched on 14 January 2026, in which "the impressive sum of R$ 2,245,235,850.24" was frozen, in an account of Daniel Vorcaro's father, Henrique Moura Vorcaro, at CBSF DTVM, "better known as REAG". The next sentence is the PF's, not the justice's, and it must be said: while the deposit-guarantee fund "bled to cover the multi-billion hole", which the police estimate at "almost 40 billion reais", the suspect was allegedly hiding from creditors and victims "more than 2 billion". The PF also points out that the main suspect "still owns private jets, as well as extensive assets abroad, including in tax havens". On the father's account, there is a separate chapter in the record, in May, and it is left for the end of this series: the father contests it, says the money is illiquid carbon credits, and the justice keeps the freeze without ruling on the merits.

There is also a detail that links this petition to another drawer, Inquiry 5.035, under the same reporting justice. The PF says the organisation hired influencers for a "DV Project" (*Projeto DV*), put into practice in December 2025, with the aim of "attacking the Central Bank's reputation" in the same period in which the federal audit court (TCU) "was signalling that it would undo the liquidation of Banco Master". Whether that is true, Inquiry 5.035 will say. Here, what matters is the argument: for the PF, the group kept acting with the suspect at liberty.

## The anatomy of a ruling

Anyone who has never read a precautionary ruling of the Supreme Court finds the structure odd. It has a report (what was requested), reasoning (why it is decided) and an operative part (what is decided). This one follows the recipe with an engineer's numbering: items 10 to 79 describe, one by one, what the police attribute to each suspect; items 80 to 94 deal with the detentions; 95 to 99, the alternative measures; 100 to 105, the companies; 106 onwards, the orders proper, including the instructions on how to make the arrests, where to take the detainees and what the judge at the custody hearing may and may not do.

The justice lists, in item 8.1, the statutory provisions at stake, and they fit in one paragraph: fraudulent management, misleading investors and irregular issuance of securities, all under Law 7.492; passive and active bribery and breach of official secrecy, under the Penal Code; criminal organisation and laundering, under their own statutes; and three crimes against the administration of justice, coercion in the course of proceedings, procedural fraud and false accusation. Note what is not there: nobody was indicted for anything in this ruling. An indictment is another document, by another author, the prosecution service. The ruling says there are "consistent indications". Indication is a word the law uses with precision and journalism with laziness.

Of the eight suspects described, four had their detention ordered: Vorcaro, Fabiano Campos Zettel, Luiz Phillipi Machado de Moraes Mourão and Marilson Roseno da Silva. Four received measures other than detention, with an ankle monitor: the Central Bank officials Paulo Sérgio Neves de Souza and Belline Santana, the company administrator Leonardo Augusto Furtado Palhares and the employee Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva. Five companies had their activities suspended indefinitely. Item 125 sends the case to the next virtual session of the Second Panel, for confirmation. The Panel confirmed it unanimously, in a session from 13 to 20 March, as a later ruling in the same record notes.

> Reading rule number two: in a precautionary ruling, the phrase "the investigations indicate" is the subject of almost everything. Who indicates is the police; who decides whether the indication is enough to detain is the judge; who decides whether it is true is a trial that does not yet exist.

## What comes in the next chapters

Chapter 2 is about the crew: what the ruling says it did, how much it cost per month and with whom. Chapter 3 is about the two Central Bank officials, the head and the deputy head of the department that supervised the bank itself, and about the consultancy firm that allegedly served as a pass-through account. Each chapter ends with the list of pages it came from. The ruling is e-Doc. 16 of Petition 15.556, and the STF's package carries the file with the number 00016 in its name. Check it. It is the only way this text is worth anything.
