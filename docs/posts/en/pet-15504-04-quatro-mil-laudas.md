Inquiry 2026.0053200 was opened on 14 May 2026 and, in September, still had no final report. What Petition 15.504 holds of it is the main file, 379 sheets up to 1 September, and fourteen annexes. This chapter is the chronicle of that progress.

## The inheritance

Police chief Albert Paulo Sérvio de Moura began by inheriting. On 4 March 2026, the third phase of Operation Compliance Zero, decided in Petition 15.562, had executed searches at the homes of the two officials, of Zettel and of Vorcaro, among others. Each search generated its own record, and on 19 May the police chief ordered eleven of them annexed to the new inquiry. Some are short and eloquent. The one for Vorcaro's house in Nova Lima, in a gated community, has six pages of photographs and one paragraph: the team arrived at 6:15 am, the caretaker said the house was empty, the wardrobes confirmed it, and the residence, with "games rooms, indoor and outdoor pools, water park, lakes, golf course, assorted courts, bowling alley, shooting range, ski simulator, music room, nightclub, gym", yielded nothing: "Nothing was collected". Belline's yielded two laptops, one of them the Central Bank's, and two phones. Zettel's, iPhones, a MacBook, eleven flash drives and a cheap phone still in its box, inside an envelope with the sender "Leonardo Palhares".

On 15 May, the police chief asked the analysis unit to examine that material "with a focus on the crimes of passive bribery, active bribery, money laundering and criminal organisation related to the BACEN officials". The result of that analysis is not in the petition.

## The first round

The summonses went out on 18 and 19 May: Ailton de Aquino for 25 May, Belline for the 26th, Paulo Sérgio for the 27th, Leonardo Palhares for the 28th, Ana Cláudia Queiroz de Paiva for 1 June, Vorcaro for 3 June.

The Central Bank's legal department replied on 18 May that the director could not come in the morning because he would be presenting the Financial Stability Report, and asked for the afternoon; he was heard at 2:30 pm. The two officials did something else: they filed petitions. Belline pointed out that he wears an ankle monitor and cannot leave São Paulo without authorisation. Paulo Sérgio, in eight pages dated 21 May, said that he had received a copy of the inquiry and of the twelve annexes the day before, "totalling no fewer than 4,260 pages", with "only 5 incomplete working days" before the statement; that his computers and phone had been under seizure since 4 March without a forensic report; that he needed his work e-mail and the Central Bank's Teams to reconstruct dates; and that he lives in Guaxupé, under electronic monitoring, and could not go to Brasília. He asked for a postponement.

The police chief replied on 27 May in a joint order, and the order is a small lesson. Return of property and copies of extractions "must be submitted to the STF"; Ailton's hearing "has already been carried out in full" and is available; the work e-mail "will be analysed at the appropriate time". And the principle: the inquiry "is an administrative procedure of a pre-trial, inquisitorial and preparatory nature, in which the adversarial principle and the right to a full defence apply in mitigated form", so that pending forensic examinations "do not constitute an obstacle to carrying out the acts, nor a reasonable ground for indefinite postponement". New summonses; Paulo Sérgio would be heard from the Federal Police unit "with jurisdiction over Guaxupé".

## Who spoke and who stayed silent

Ana Cláudia, on 1 June, and Zettel, on 2 June, remained silent, accompanied by lawyers. Márcio Contador Camargo, the official whose name appears in the metadata of the farm contract, was heard on 11 June. Palhares, after one postponement, on 15 June. Belline did not appear either on 26 May or on 16 June; the police chief issued a "third and final summons", recording that the absence would be "interpreted as a legitimate exercise of the right to silence". Paulo Sérgio's wife and brother, both under investigation because of the farm and the house, asked not to travel to Belo Horizonte, attaching a medical report on the brother; the police chief granted it and arranged a room at the Guaxupé courthouse. She was heard on 7 July; the brother remained silent on 9 July; Paulo Sérgio spoke on 10 July, for hours.

There was also the matter of the concierge. Léo Serrano, Vorcaro's travel fixer, asked for access to the inquiry. The police chief refused, on 12 June, in an order that explains why he would be heard as a witness and not as a suspect: the elements "indicate that his involvement is confined, in principle, to the handling of logistical arrangements for international trips paid for by DANIEL BUENO VORCARO", and a witness "does not mount a defence: he occupies the position of a means of evidence". Serrano was heard on 24 August.

Belline finally appeared on 25 August, in São Paulo, in person. His lawyer had asked for a videoconference "for convenience"; the police chief refused on 21 July, saying that a statement taken outside an official setting "compromises procedural security, the traceability of the act and the integrity of the record", and that convenience is not a reason. Vorcaro was scheduled for 27 August, by video, from the custody unit of the 19th Battalion of the Federal District Military Police. At 10:19 am, the unit reported by WhatsApp that it had no internet; at 10:31 am, a private confirmed by telephone that there was no technician on site. The lawyer entered the virtual room alone and asked for a new date. The police clerk certified everything, hour by hour, and the hearing took place the following day, in four video files.

On 3 August, the police chief opened the next round: he asked the Central Bank to present, as witnesses, its president Gabriel Galípolo and, once again, Ailton; and ordered the summoning of André Esteves, of BTG, and Roberto Campos Neto, the bank's former president. None of those statements appears in the petition up to September.

## The hard drive

A side episode shows what, in practice, the "access to evidence" guaranteed by Binding Precedent 14 amounts to. Belline's defence asked the Supreme Court, in Petition 15.562, for a copy of the contents of the seized devices. The justice asked the police. The police chief replied, on 3 July, that it was "technically possible" to produce an authenticated copy with a hash, provided the defence brought "new recording media, sealed in the original factory packaging, accompanied by the corresponding receipt". On 12 August, the lawyer handed over a 1-terabyte external hard drive, Knup brand, bought days earlier at a shop in Brasília for R$ 589, receipt attached. The drive was sealed under number C0002523477. On 14 August, at 1:56 pm, the seal was broken, and a police officer copied onto it two compressed files: the forensic report on the two phones, extracted with Cellebrite, and the report on one of the laptops. The other laptop, the Central Bank's, was protected by BitLocker with a pre-boot PIN and "it was not possible to carry out the extraction". Once the hashes were checked, the drive received a new seal, C0002530694, and was returned on 18 August. Nine pages of report to copy two files, and that is how it should be.

## What leaked

Twice, in the inquiry, the seal failed, and both times are documented by the person who felt harmed. On 12 August, Paulo Sérgio filed with the Supreme Court that he had been "surprised by the media disclosure" of Ailton's statement, with headlines such as "we trusted no one", and that a magazine had linked him to a surveillance group he was not part of. On 26 August, he came back: now the concierge's statement, of the 24th, had leaked, with the figure of US$ 38,000. He asked, both times, that the seal on his own statement of 10 July be lifted, "both in the form of the audiovisual media and of the written statement", so that his version too would reach the "court of public opinion". Mendonça sent the file to the PGR for comment on 14 August. The PGR had not replied in the record by September.

It is worth noting who was complaining about a leak: a suspect whom, in the previous chapter, the police accuse of leaking. And it is worth noting his argument, which is not a legal one: if the seal protects only one side, the seal is an instrument against the defence. The justice, for now, has not replied.

## The Central Bank

Two replies from the Central Bank complete the picture. The internal-affairs office reported, on 9 June, that the two asset inquiries had been concluded on 4 and 5 March, "on account of the existence, in principle, of indications of disciplinary offences", and that the bank's president had sent the case to the federal comptroller (CGU) on 10 March; that the officials have been suspended since 8 January, first by decision of the director, then, since 4 March, by order of the Supreme Court; and that a full copy of the inquiries, "including the ancillary files in which the documents protected by tax secrecy are enclosed", had already been sent to the court on 11 March.

And, on the property side, the notary registries: Muzambinho's sent the farm's title record and the 2021 deed that transferred it to Pipe "and as a capital contribution"; Guaxupé's received the request for the title record of the house sold to Noah on 30 January 2024, with an addendum on 5 July 2024.

## The deadline

On 6 July, the deadline having expired, the police chief listed what was missing: the four statements from the family and the two officials, a new hearing of Palhares on "the relations between VARAJO CONSULTORIA and the contract of BELLINE SANTANA", the hearing of Vorcaro "from the prison unit", and "bank and financial-flow analyses" that depended on data requested in another petition and not yet received in the Simba system. He asked for sixty days. The justice granted them, on 13 July. On 1 September, the police chief sent "the continuation of the documentation" and asked for more time; on 4 September, the justice granted another sixty days. Until November, therefore, no final report.

The inquiry has eight formal suspects, with full access to the file since 13 August, six substantive statements, two silences and four witnesses still to be heard, among them the president of the Central Bank. What the statements say is the subject of the two final chapters.

> Reading rule number thirty-one: silence is a right, and the police chief writes as much in his own order. But notice the distribution: those who stayed silent are the ones the police say moved the money; those who spoke are the ones who say they did not know where the money came from. Conclude nothing from this. Just note it.
