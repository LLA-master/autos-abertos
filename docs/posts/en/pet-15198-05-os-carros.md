After June 2026, Petition 15.198 became what the reporting justice wanted it to be: the drawer of precautionary measures. And a precautionary measure, in practice, is people asking for things back. Between February and August, six requests for the return of property came in and six were refused, with the same text, sometimes literally the same paragraph. This chapter is about them, because it is where the Banco Master case touches material life: garages, safes, financing accounts. And because the repetition, here, is not laziness on the judge's part; it is doctrine.

## The garage in Belo Horizonte

Henrique Moura Vorcaro, Daniel's father, asked on 2 February for the return of six vehicles seized at his home on 14 January. The seizure record, which the July ruling transcribes, lists the rest: seven iPads, two Hublot Big Bang watches, a Montblanc, an HYT Trinity Five, a Cartier Tank. And the cars: an Aston Martin DBX 707, a Jeep Wrangler, a Bentley Continental GT Speed, a Range Rover Sport SVR, a Toyota Hilux SW4 and a BMW X5 M50i. The petition, signed by the criminal lawyer Eugênio Pacelli, argues that Henrique is "a peripheral figure, with no direct imputation of criminal conduct", included "by reason of personal and family ties"; that nobody "sought to state or even suggest that the seized vehicles were the proceeds of crime"; that the seizure was being kept up "by procedural inertia"; and that retention was causing "depreciation, deterioration and economic loss". He asked for the cars back or, at the least, possession as their court-appointed custodian (*fiel depositário*).

Thiago Assumpção Henriques, with the same law firm and the same filing adapted, asked for two Porsches, a Macan and a Panamera, both under fiduciary lien, that is, financed and still in the name of the lending bank. An additional argument, presented in August: he "remains strictly up to date with the obligations assumed under the two financing contracts, including after the seizure", paying for goods that "deteriorate in State custody": "discharge and loss of the battery, deformation of the tyres from the static weight on a single point of contact, sedimentation of fluids". It is the most precise description, in the whole archive, of what happens to a car left standing in a yard.

## The others

Felipe Cançado Vorcaro, the brother, asked in March for the lifting of all restraints: "21 properties, 2 vehicles and sums in bank accounts". He said he "never took part in the management or the shareholding of, or had any financial relationship with", Master; that the two real-estate firms the police used as a link, Três Vales and Trancoso Eco, "never operated" and "were duly dissolved in 2022"; and that the freeze prevented him from paying his financing, "creating, paradoxically, a real risk of destroying the very assets it seeks to preserve". In the alternative, he asked that the freeze be limited to R$ 1,604,420.41.

Maurício Quadrado, Luis Fernando de Almeida and Flávio Aguetoni, of the FIDC Alvarinho, gave the most detailed explanation of all: Bello Pactum, they said, "was structured exclusively to operationalise the payment of labour settlements relating to BANIF", the Portuguese bank that Master bought, "through the issuance of commercial notes subscribed by the FIDC Alvarinho", and the sums "would have been used in full to pay labour entitlements". In other words: it was not diversion, it was a settlement payroll. The police replied that the explanation "corroborates the indications": using a FIDC "backed by capital raised from investors to absorb liabilities" from the purchase of BANIF is to transfer to the fund "the risks of the transaction", with an impact "on the backing of the CDBs and on the deposit-guarantee fund". The same transaction, read from two sides, is either paying workers or engineering risk. It is the kind of dispute only a trial settles, and this one has not yet begun.

Fernando Alves Vieira asked for thirteen firearms, "duly registered", which he kept as a collector, sport shooter and hunter, seized, he said, only because the Army's system was down during the search; two cars, a Pajero and a Range Rover; and the correction of a judicial deposit of R$ 184,750.00 seized at his home, which appeared registered in the name of "a third party unconnected to the record", plus information on the fate of some foreign currency. A company, Komerco Consultoria, asked for a Hilux it says it uses "in the ordinary course of its business activities".

## The answer

All refused. Felipe's, Quadrado and company's and Vieira's on 19 May; Henrique's and Thiago's on 20 July; the new requests by Thiago and Komerco, on 5 August, were not even decided: the reporting justice ordered them docketed as separate petitions, being "matter foreign to the limits decided in the present proceeding".

The core of the answer fits into four sentences that repeat from ruling to ruling. One: "the release of seized goods is an exceptional measure, admitted only when it is shown, unequivocally, that they bear no relation to the facts under investigation". Two: the goods are restrained "precisely because, on a summary assessment, there are strong indications that they may constitute the product, proceeds or instrument of unlawful activities, or, at least, goods acquired or maintained with funds whose origin is still under investigation". Three: the discussion about lawful origin "refers to the merits of the investigation", and "any conclusion on the matter would be premature". Four: appointing the suspect as custodian "would amount to de facto enjoyment of the presumable product of a criminal offence".

On the financed Porsches, the July ruling has a sentence that serves as a general rule: "the filing of the vehicle's registration and licensing certificate is not sufficient, on its own, to prove the lawful origin of the funds used in its acquisition". And on luxury cars in general, the MPF observed, and the justice accepted, that Toffoli's original ruling already authorised "the search and seizure of luxury objects", and that "high-luxury models, such as those of the Aston Martin, Bentley and Porsche marques, fit the proposed definition". Luxury, here, is not an adjective; it is a procedural category.

There is a paragraph that appears identically in four rulings and that says more about the reporting justice's philosophy than any other: "reimbursement of the public purse or the forfeiture of goods cannot even be legally categorised as penalties or sanctions. They are measures aimed at restoring depleted public assets, and they take precedence over the interest of any creditor. Should there be a conviction, the possessor of those goods cannot dispose of them, since they should not even be regarded as assets forming part of his estate." It is the same logic that, in the other petition, kept the assets of a dead suspect frozen: to repair is not to punish, and that is why it passes to heirs and comes before creditors. Whoever disagrees with that premise disagrees with everything that came after.

## What was actually answered

Not everything was a blanket refusal. To Vieira, the reporting justice explained the mystery of the deposit in another person's name: the Federal Police reported, with documents, that the operation's judicial account was opened "on the occasion of the first deposit", tied to the first target processed, and that "all the judicial deposits of the operation" carry the same name in the "defendant" field, including those of other suspects. It was not an error against Vieira; it was the form. And the foreign currency, the police reported on their "official word" (*fé pública*), was not deposited electronically: it was "physically delivered" to a branch of Caixa in Belo Horizonte on 20 January. The justice held the "destination duly proven". Vieira and the Alvarinho trio filed internal appeals; in June, the appeals went to the PGR. When the seal fell, they were undecided.

As for the thirteen guns, the police said they "remain necessary to the investigation, the technical and forensic examinations intended to check for possible tampering still being pending", and the reporting justice accepted that. The reader may find it odd that a collector's registered guns should interest an investigation into FIDCs. The record does not explain the connection; it says only that the forensic work is not finished. This text records the oddity and the gap, in that order.

## What remains

Six months after the operation, no property seized in the second phase had been returned by a ruling in this petition. The pattern is clear and, being clear, is debatable: as long as the investigation is not over, there is no way to prove that an asset is clean, and as long as that is not proven, the asset stays. It is an inversion that the law of precautionary measures admits and that the defence calls, rightly or wrongly, "anticipated confiscation". Komerco used exactly that expression. The reporting justice answered by ordering it docketed separately.

The main investigation, remember, left this drawer in June and went into a closed inquiry. What is left here is the bill: cars draining their batteries in a yard, financing paid on time for goods the owner cannot use, an estate administrator who must be admitted to the docket to discuss the assets of a dead man. It is the part of the case that does not make the headlines and that, for those inside it, is the only one that exists every day.

> Reading rule number eleven: "summary assessment" (*cognição sumária*) is the expression that sends back the most refused requests. It means "I do not know yet", and "I do not know yet", in an asset freeze, means "it stays with me".
