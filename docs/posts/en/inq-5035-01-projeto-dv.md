Inquiry 5.035 is the smallest of the fifteen proceedings in this archive: 85 filings and 321 pages, a single substantive decision by the reporting justice, three procedural orders. It is also the only one that is not about money, portfolios, funds or banks, but about opinion. It investigates whether someone paid, or tried to pay, influencers to say on social media that the Central Bank was wrong to liquidate Banco Master. The hypothesis has a code name, "Project DV", and the initials, according to the police, "refer to the suspect who controls Banco Master". This series has three chapters, because the subject does not stretch to more, and this site will not stretch what the record does not have.

The usual caveat, reinforced here. The inquiry was opened to investigate the crime of "obstructing the investigation of a criminal organisation", and nobody has been formally named a suspect or charged in the public filings. The people heard were heard as witnesses. The influencers listed by police intelligence are not, in the filings this site has read, formal suspects, and for that reason will not be named here, except those who presented themselves to the proceeding through a lawyer. The link between the campaign and the banker is, as far as the record shows, an acronym in a contract.

## The allegation

On 6 January 2026, the city councillor of Erechim, in Rio Grande do Sul, Rony Gabriel, owner of a profile with 1.7 million followers, posted a video. He said that, on 20 December, "a company" had contacted his aide: "We are doing reputation management and crisis management work for a big executive and we have been hiring profiles that take a stand to help us in this political dispute we are waging against the system. It is a case of national repercussion, big people, the left and the centrão involved." There was a non-disclosure agreement with a penalty of R$ 800,000. The proposal, he said, was to make videos saying "that Banco Master was a victim of the Central Bank". He declined and, on seeing "several right-wing influencers attacking the Central Bank" on the same day, decided to speak out.

Three days later, G1 reported that a content creator had received R$ 7,840 for a single post on 19 December, that the money had come from the account of Thiago Miranda, owner of Miranda Comunicação, "also known as Agência MiThi", and that the contract on offer had as its parties Miranda Comunicação and a company called Olivetto Comunicação, charged with sending "the articles, links and guidance on the topic to be covered". The report noted, on the basis of tax-authority data, that the partner in Miranda Comunicação also administered the company of the journalist Léo Dias. A column in the newspaper O Globo added: offers of R$ 2 million for three months to an influencer with more than a million followers, R$ 250,000 to the smaller ones, eight posts a month.

That was all, and only that, that the Federal Police had on 12 January, when a police officer at the Special Inquiries Division in Brasília drafted police intelligence report (IPJ) 82.854/2026: ten pages summarising "three reports from two news portals". The document says of itself that it "does not present an exhaustive means".

## The 22 profiles

The next day, the Federal Police's Intelligence Directorate, through its counter-intelligence coordination, delivered Informação 97.765/2026, 73 pages. The head of the division had ordered, on 8 January, "that profiles and posts possibly engaged" in the attacks "be identified". The result is a list of 22 Instagram profiles, almost all of them gossip and celebrity pages, with "posts at close intervals, attacking the Central Bank's conduct". The largest of them has 25 million followers; another, belonging to a television presenter, 24 million; several exceed seven million. The posts, for the most part, date from 27 and 28 December 2025, and a good share of them, the document records, "is currently deleted". Intelligence grouped the profiles by media agency and, for each one, attached what it called the registration data of the probable person responsible.

This site has read those pages and will not reproduce them. They contain the parents' names, date of birth, identity document, "unconfirmed" address, e-mail and telephone of more than twenty people who, in the inquiry, are not suspects, were not heard and were not served notice. A reader who wants to know who the profiles are will find the answer in the press of the time, which named them. What the record adds to the press is the confirmation that the police looked at the same profiles, and the information, this one relevant, that most of the posts vanished before the police arrived.

## The opening order

On 28 January 2026, a police chief at the Coordination of Inquiries before the Higher Courts opened the police inquiry. The summary of the facts says that, in the course of Operation Compliance Zero, "an attempt to manipulate the flow of information about the case on social media was found to have occurred", through "the publication of news to the effect that the suspect Daniel Bueno Vorcaro had allegedly hired a marketing company to carry out negative publicity and/or defamation of the Central Bank before society, with the aim of hindering the progress of the investigations, and also of helping the businessman to influence the decisions under way, both before the Supreme Federal Court and before the Federal Court of Accounts". Note the wording: "publication of news to the effect that". The opening order launches an inquiry into what the press said happened, which is legitimate and common, but it is worth reading it that way.

The opening order also fixes what the police knew about the contracts: "the contracts were allegedly offered in the name of the company Miranda Comunicação, also known as Agência MiThi, owned by Thiago Miranda". And it orders notice to be served, first, on the councillor and on the journalist Juliana Moreira Leite, who had also posted a video saying she had declined the same proposal; then, on "those responsible for the other profiles listed". On the same day, the inquiry was redistributed "for equitable readjustment of workloads", and, the next day, handed to police chief Ramon Santos Morais, who has presided over it since. On 11 February, a police clerk certified that the item about the other profiles would not be carried out for the time being: the police chief would hear "first, the witnesses Rony and Juliana, and then indicate the remaining persons involved to be heard". Up to the last filing in the archive, that remainder has not been indicated.

## The split

Still on 28 January, three police chiefs from São Paulo, the same ones as in Petition 15.198, asked Justice Dias Toffoli for "the splitting of this proceeding": an order in Inquiry 5.026 had said that "the facts relating to the investigations of the so-called influencers were already the object" of the Petition, and the police, "in order to avoid procedural turmoil", wanted a record of their own, "whose specific object" would be "the investigation of the coordinated media offensive, identified by the press as 'Project DV'". The Prosecutor General, Paulo Gonet, agreed on 30 January: the facts "require deeper examination", and "investigation in a separate proceeding is a fitting and necessary solution", "as it involves an autonomous set of facts". On 6 February, he said again that "until the jurisdiction to supervise the investigation is settled, the investigations directly derived from it must be kept before the Supreme Federal Court".

On 9 February, Toffoli ordered the police request and the PGR's opinions detached from Petition 15.198, "re-docketing them as a separate Inquiry to be distributed to me by prevention" (*prevenção*). In the same order, he granted "the sharing of the evidence obtained in the 1st phase of Operation Compliance Zero with the police inquiry in question" and the defence teams' access "to the elements of evidence already documented". Four days later, he would cease to be reporting justice of all of it. Inquiry 5.035 was born, therefore, as one of Toffoli's last acts in the case, and was inherited, like the others, by André Mendonça.

## What the new reporting justice said

The only substantive decision in the record of 5.035 is, in fact, a copy. On 19 February 2026, in Petition 15.198, Mendonça ruled on the forensic examination of "about 100 electronic devices" and on the police's workflow, and ordered the decision transferred "to all the proceedings under my reporting in which the investigation relates to 'Operation Compliance Zero'". The reader of the series on Petition 15.198 knows the text: the principles "of preserving secrecy and functionality", cited from the justice's own article, and the rule that "the opening of any new investigation or inquiry must first be expressly and reasonedly requested from this Reporting Justice". The chronological irony is worth recording: the influencers' inquiry, created ten days earlier by another justice at the request of the police, reached the new reporting justice already carrying the warning that new inquiries would need his prior authorisation.

After that, the reporting justice issued three procedural orders: on 31 March, to extend the inquiry by 60 days and to grant access to Thiago Miranda's lawyers, "subject to the restrictions inherent to the sealed nature of the proceeding", and "as of now" to "the lawyers of the other suspects who come to prove that condition". The PGR took note on 1 April. That is all. The rest of the record is the police at work and the lawyers knocking at the door, which are the two chapters that follow.

> Reading rule number twenty-two: an inquiry opened on the strength of news reports starts out in debt to the press and pays the debt by hearing the sources. Until it hears anyone beyond them, what it has is the news report, with a stamp on it.
