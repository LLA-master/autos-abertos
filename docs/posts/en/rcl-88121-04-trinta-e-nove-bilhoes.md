The interim order of 9 December ordered the Central Bank to hand the Supreme Court "the documentation and the information relating to the administrative proceedings under way". On 22 December 2025, the same day as the final decision, the Central Bank's legal department entered into the complaint a full copy of an internal proceeding of 751 pages. The central document is Report 4875/2025, of 15 December, the result of a "Special Verification" at BRB, signed by a technical manager and a supervisor from the Belo Horizonte unit of the bank-supervision department. This chapter is about that document, which is the most complete source, in the public record, on the size of the business between the two banks. The numbers are the Central Bank's; the interpretation, where there is any, is flagged.

## The scope and the caveat

The stated aim was "to assess the quality and management of BRB's loan portfolio, with a focus on the operations acquired from Banco Master from 01/07/2024". Base date: 31 July 2025, with movements up to October and information declared by BRB about November. The caveat comes in the very first paragraph: "during the execution of the work, the financial statements for the whole acquisition period had not been delivered to the Central Bank, which made it impossible to apply the audit techniques in full". The supervisors also record "the simplification of the scope", "motivated by urgency". It is a report done in a hurry, about a bank that had not yet delivered all its accounts, and it says so of itself.

## The numbers

"Between July 2024 and October 2025, BRB acquired loan portfolios originated by Banco Master and by companies linked to it, totalling R$ 33.9 billion." The report stresses that the acquisitions grew "without prior and adequate analysis of the quality of the assets". In 2025, "substitutions of portfolios initially acquired intensified, owing to documentary inconsistencies and origination defects", and the "return/resale process" "amounted to R$ 15.7 billion in financial value". Through it, BRB "began to receive assets such as CRIs, FIDC quotas, equity funds and concentrated credit operations". All of this "in parallel with BRB's failed attempt to acquire Master's equity, refused by the Central Bank on 03/09/2025". On 15 October 2025, the Central Bank imposed on BRB a "Preventive Prudential Measure" that "ordered the immediate halt of operations to acquire or exchange financial assets", allowing only substitution.

The table consolidating the assets, with "book balances at 30/09/2025", is the part of the report that deserves to be read line by line. The site summarises it, in billions of reais, with the acquisition value reported by BRB and the book balance declared by the bank:

| Block | Acquisition | Balance at 30.9.25 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Credcesta (payroll-deduction card, assignor Master) | 26.06 | 8.79 |
| Other retail portfolios (FGTS, payroll loans, revolving credit etc.) | 5.55 | 3.68 |
| Loans to companies (CCBs assigned by Master) | 2.59 | 2.21 |
| Fund quotas received in substitution or in payment | 4.75 | 5.23 |
| Real-estate receivables certificates (CRIs) | 1.23 | 1.36 |
| **Total** | **39.74** | **21.28** |

The R$ 39.7 billion of acquisitions is larger than the R$ 33.9 billion cited in the text because the table includes the assets received in exchange. The R$ 21.3 billion balance is what remained on BRB's balance sheet at the end of September. Between one column and the other lie amortisations, returns and, in the case of Credcesta, a drop from 26 to 8.8 billion that the report does not break down. The notes explain where each number came from: the retail portfolios, from BRB's own answers to requests for information; the CCBs, from the bank's executive memoranda; the funds and CRIs, "given in payment in kind", with the remark that "there is no record of the quotas' values having been updated".

Some names in that table reappear elsewhere in the archive. Among the loans to companies is a R$ 78 million note to "Cemitérios e Crematórios SP SPE", received "in substitution for the operations previously acquired"; it is the concessionaire of São Paulo's cemeteries which, in July 2026, a congresswoman would ask the reporting justice of the inquiry to investigate. Among the funds are Kyra, Naples, Cartago and Tessália, which add up to R$ 2.5 billion and about which the report says something that deserves a paragraph of its own.

## The swaps

"Among the funds received (Kyra FIA, Naples FIM, Cartago FIA and Tessália FIP), according to BRB, there were swap instruments with positive results, totalling R$ 2.24 billion receivable, but pending payment, and that situation is not yet reflected in the accounting records of the said funds." Further on: the swaps were "intended as protection against adverse changes in the prices of the funds' assets", and their "receivables, in the amount of R$ 2,237,387,000.00, were not settled by the counterparty". In plain terms: BRB received fund quotas in payment of Master's debts; the value of those quotas depended on insurance against a fall; and whoever was supposed to pay the insurance did not pay. The report orders BRB to determine the "fair value of the equity quotas of all the funds" and to deduct "the amounts arising from derivative instruments not honoured". Who the counterparty was, the report does not say.

## What the supervisors saw at BRB

The findings section is short and hard, and for that reason this site transcribes it almost in full. "BRB's structure proved limited for absorbing large-volume operations with characteristics different from its own portfolio." "In the case of the retail portfolio, there was no verification of the payroll deductions with the public bodies, nor analysis of the geographical location of the borrowers, concentrated in Bahia." "As for the corporate portfolios and CRIs, the information available for analysis was basically supplied by Master itself, since the clients dealt exclusively with it." "The acquired loans remained domiciled at Master, which was responsible for passing the payments on to BRB, but the controls over those receipts were precarious, carried out on a consolidated basis and without individualised information."

On governance: "several executive memoranda warned of material risks and breaches of limits, such as concentration by counterparty, economic sector, concentrated exposures, secured operations with individuals and treasury limits. Despite this, the operations were approved by the statutory bodies." And, in another passage, "such operations were approved by the competent decision levels on an exceptional basis, despite the existence of warnings from the technical areas". On risk: the probabilities of default calculated for the portfolios bought from Master were "lower than those applied to similar products in BRB's own portfolio". The state-owned bank estimated that a third party's credit, which it had not checked, was safer than its own.

## What the Central Bank ordered

Four orders, in round numbers. Provision in full "the assets identified with origination defects", R$ 2.64 billion, "covering the Credcesta portfolio originated by Tirreno (R$ 2,055,943,631.59) and instalments of contracts with origination defects (R$ 580,878,694.34)". Mark the fund quotas to fair value, deducting the R$ 2.24 billion in unpaid swaps. Review the risk modelling, using, in the meantime, the losses on its own portfolio. And classify as "problem assets" the loans to companies whose payment BRB cannot demonstrate, given that some of them had "single maturity ('bullet')", "long grace periods" and "extensions". All of it accompanied by a "Solution Plan" within 30 days, execution within six months, and bimonthly reports from the independent auditor.

Two billion six hundred million is the number the supervisor put, in December 2025, on the part of the Tirreno portfolio it considered unsound. It is not twelve billion, nor sixteen, nor eighteen, which are the numbers of the police, of the MPF and of the judge. Nor is it zero, which is the defence's number. This site does not know which is right; it knows they are measurements of different things, and that whoever compares them without saying what each one measures is misleading someone.

## The attachments

The 58 attachments are the correspondence between BRB and the supervisor, and they tell a parallel story: the Central Bank was asking from early on. On 30 August 2024, by e-mail, it asked for details of the July assignments; BRB replied on 6 September, listing a single purchase from Master, R$ 673 million of "payroll-deduction benefit card" with a R$ 296 million premium. In November 2024, a new request, about September; the reply lists three purchases from Master in one week. In January 2025, a request about November and December; the reply runs to fourteen pages and shows the business at cruising speed: dozens of Credcesta assignments, almost all at 27.12% a year, with premiums reaching R$ 370 million in a single operation on 26 December. In the first three weeks of January 2025, R$ 1.5 billion in three assignments. The letters are signed by managers, superintendents and the finance director, Dario Oswaldo Garcia Junior, who would be removed in November; one of the managers has the same name as a suspect who, in August 2026, would ask for access to the inquiry file.

What those attachments show, beyond the numbers, is that supervision was contemporaneous. The Central Bank asked month by month, and BRB answered with tables, contracts, proof of registration at B3, internal approvals. The question the December 2025 report leaves hanging, and which no filing in the archive answers, is why those tables, which reached the supervisor weeks late, only turned into a Prudential Measure in October 2025.

> Reading rule number twenty: when five institutions give five numbers for the same loss, choose neither the largest nor the smallest. Ask what each one measured. In this case, one measured transfers, another measured presumed fraud, and the supervisor measured what it ordered provisioned.
